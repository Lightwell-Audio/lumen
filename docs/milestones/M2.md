# Milestone 2 — Audio Engine, Devices & Transport: plan

**Status:** In progress · **Started:** 2026-10-06 · **Approved:** 2026-10-06
Results so far: [`docs/MANUAL_TESTS.md`](../MANUAL_TESTS.md) (MT-AUDIO-1 to -6)
and evidence in [`docs/DECISIONS.md`](../DECISIONS.md) (ADR-0006, ADR-0016).

Milestone 2 makes Lumen reliably play audio: a production device layer for
ASIO, WASAPI and the macOS and Linux hosts; a real-time engine with a
sample-accurate transport; and the simplest timeline that can play audio
files, with the transport bar, metronome, meters and device selector a
producer needs around it.

Unlike M1, everything here is production code under `crates/`. M1 prototypes
are the starting point, not the result.

| ID | Risk | Impact if late | Addressed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Drivers misbehave on unplug, reset and sleep: silent stalls, failed reopens, false recoveries. | Audio stops mid-session with no way back | Slice 1 ✅ (supervisor, watchdog, confirmed restore; MT-AUDIO-1, -2, -6) |

## 3. Slices in detail

Each slice states its goal, what gets built, and the checks that close it.
Hardware results are recorded in `docs/MANUAL_TESTS.md`.

### Slice 1 — `lumen-audio` device layer ✅
- **Goal:** One device interface for every driver model, and a supervisor that keeps audio running through resets, configuration changes and device loss.
- **Build:** `crates/lumen-audio`: device and stream types, the `Backend` trait, `AudioSystem` (control thread, lock-free status and meters, events), a scriptable fake backend, and the native ASIO backend grown from P-ASIO.
- **Pass:** supervisor tests for start, reset, unplug and return, stall, configuration fallback and Audio Off. On hardware, unplug and replug recover with exactly one `Lost` and one `Restored`, and driver resets reopen at the new settings.
- **Result (2026-10-06):** 9 supervisor tests. MT-AUDIO-1 found a false `Restored` while the device was still gone; fixed by confirming the device delivers audio for 1 s before reporting `Restored`. MT-AUDIO-1, -2 and -3 pass on the Focusrite.

### Slice 2 — WASAPI, CoreAudio and Linux backends ✅
- **Goal:** WASAPI shared mode (and exclusive, a should-have) on Windows; macOS and Linux behind the same interface.
- **Build:** `lumen-audio::wasapi` on the `wasapi` crate; `lumen-audio::cpal_host` on cpal (CoreAudio, ALSA); shared sample conversion; the `audio_tone` example with `--driver` (ADR-0016).
- **Pass:** shared and exclusive output at the expected callback rate on real hardware; device loss and Windows format changes recover; macOS and Linux compile and pass unit tests in CI.
- **Result (2026-10-06):** shared 480 frames at 48 kHz; exclusive down to 3 ms. MT-AUDIO-4 found the Focusrite's exclusive-mode events running its clock at 60% of real time; fixed by checking the device clock at start and falling back to polling. MT-AUDIO-4, -5 and -6 pass. Input for WASAPI and cpal moved to slice 8.

### Slice 3 — `lumen-engine`: transport and timing ✅
- **Goal:** A real-time engine, grown from P-ENGINE, that runs inside the device callback and owns the transport.
- **Build:** play, stop, pause, loop and seek; tempo and time-signature maps on the tick time base; loop and punch regions as transport state (recording through punch is M9); sample-accurate event scheduling; lock-free commands from the UI and position and state reported back.
- **Pass:** zero heap operations in `process` (`lumen-rt` tests); RealtimeSanitizer job in Linux CI; identical output for every block size; loop wrap and seek land on the exact sample; tempo changes keep bar and beat positions exact.
- **Result (2026-10-06):** `lumen-engine` with 13 transport tests (exact loop wraps at block sizes 1 to 10,000, tempo and rate changes keep the bar and beat), a seqlock test and 5,000 mixed blocks with zero heap operations. MT-ENGINE-1 passes by ear on the Focusrite. RealtimeSanitizer is live in CI: the engine passes under it, and three canaries (file open, held lock, allocation) are caught. The first canaries (a sleep, an uncontended lock) were undetectable by RTSan, found by probing under WSL (ADR-0010). Time-signature changes in the project file arrive in M3 (ADR-0017).

### Slice 4 — Transport bar with vector icons ✅
- **Goal:** The transport bar the spec describes, with crisp vector icons (owner request).
- **Build:** play, pause, stop, record, loop and metronome icons as Vello paths; tempo and time-signature fields; an editable position display in bars and beats and in minutes and seconds; tooltips with shortcuts; Space for play and stop.
- **Pass:** controls drive the slice 3 engine; icons stay sharp at 100%, 150% and 200% scale in both themes; every control has an accessible name and works from the keyboard.
- **Result (2026-10-06):** vector icons on egui's painter (ADR-0018), transport bar driving the engine on the system default output or `--audio`, Space / Shift+Space / Home, click-to-type position, meter-aware ruler and grid, playhead and loop range. Screenshots at 100/150/200% found the panel toggles pushed off-screen at 200% (fixed). MT-APP-2 found a click-to-playhead delay (fixed; measured 14–19 ms with the new `LUMEN_TRACE` tooling). Dark theme only: the light theme arrives with the design system in M3.

### Slice 5 — Click-to-locate on the ruler ✅
- **Goal:** The first part of the owner's Ableton-style scrubbing request.
- **Build:** clicking the ruler moves the playhead, while stopped and while playing, snapped to the grid with a modifier to bypass snapping.
- **Pass:** the locate lands on the exact tick and sample. The scrub strip, audible scrubbing and ruler drag-zoom follow in M3.
- **Result (2026-10-06):** clicking the ruler locates to the nearest line of a zoom-adaptive grid (bar down to 1/16 of a beat, at least 12 px), counted from the bar start so time-signature changes stay right; Alt places freely; a ghost line previews the target. 4 snapping tests; MT-APP-3 passes. Run 1 tested a stale build: the owner's and the agent's builds went to different folders, now unified (`target\`, started with `cargo run`).

### Slice 6 — Audio file playback on a simple timeline (plan: awaiting approval)
- **Goal:** The simplest timeline that plays audio files.
- **Proposed design:**
  1. **`lumen-media` crate:** decode with symphonia (MPL-2.0, owner decision: unmodified, licence notice shipped, cargo-deny exception) to `f32`. Formats: WAV, AIFF, FLAC, MP3, Ogg Vorbis. AAC/M4A left out for now (codec licensing to check first).
  2. **Sample-rate conversion** with rubato (MIT/Apache-2.0, high-quality sinc), done once per file in the background to the device's rate. When the device rate changes (MT-AUDIO-6), files are converted again in the background; affected clips stay silent until ready.
  3. **Audio in memory for M2:** each converted file is held in RAM, shared between the clips that use it, and the audio thread only reads from memory. No disk I/O or decoding ever runs there. Cost: about 11 MB per stereo minute at 48 kHz, so a few hundred MB for a typical song. Disk streaming (for very large sessions) and the recording writer arrive together in M9. The clip player is built so streaming can replace the memory source without touching the engine.
  4. **Import runs in the background** with progress and cancel; the UI never waits.
  5. **Clips:** an audio track with clips (start in ticks, offset and length in samples, gain), swapped into the engine like the timeline. Clips start at a musical position and play at the file's own speed (no warping: a should-have, later). Each clip gets a 2 ms fade at its edges, and playback gets a 2 ms fade-in after every play, locate and loop wrap, so nothing clicks.
  6. **Adding files before the browser (M3):** drag files from Explorer onto the arrangement (spec §6.2, a must-have). They land at the drop position, snapped to the grid. Also `lumen --open-audio <file>`. Clips are drawn as coloured blocks with their name; waveforms, editing, undo and save/load arrive in M3.
- **Pass:**
  - Clips start on their exact sample, at every block size and across loop wraps and locates (golden render tests).
  - Offline render matches real-time playback.
  - Zero heap operations in the audio thread with clips playing (`lumen-rt` and RTSan).
  - Decoding and conversion tests: format fixtures, length and pitch kept through 44.1 ↔ 48 kHz.
  - 64 stereo clips at 128 frames well inside the CPU budget (measured).
  - By ear (MT-MEDIA-1): long files gapless and glitch-free at 64–128 frames on the Focusrite, with clean loops and jumps.

### Slice 7 — Metronome, test tone, meters, Audio On/Off, panic
- **Goal:** The everyday audio controls and health indicators.
- **Build:** metronome with an accented downbeat, volume and count-in; test tone; CPU load meter; a dropout counter driven by the device clock (late or missing blocks, which slice 2 showed the load meters cannot see); Audio On/Off; panic.
- **Pass:** the metronome is sample-accurate across tempo and time-signature changes; the dropout counter catches a starved device; panic silences everything at once.

### Slice 8 — Device selector
- **Goal:** Choose and configure the audio device inside the app.
- **Build:** driver type, input and output device, sample rate, buffer size, input, output and round-trip latency, channel selection with names, the ASIO control panel; driver name, version and clock sources; driver queries in a sandboxed child process; settings that persist with graceful fallback at start-up; the lost-device and busy-device states in the UI; input for WASAPI and cpal.
- **Pass:** changing driver, device, rate or buffer at run time never crashes and falls back to a working configuration on failure; a missing or busy device shows a clear message with a way forward.
