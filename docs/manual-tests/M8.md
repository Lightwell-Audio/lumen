# Drum maps, the drum editor and built-in devices: manual tests

Checks for M8's drum half and device half, added as each becomes reachable
(`docs/milestones/M8.md`).

## MT-DRUM-1 — Drum maps: attach, swap, detach and convert (D0), and the editor (D1)

**State:** Not run. Branch: `m8-sd0-drum-map-model` for steps 1 to 11 (9 to
11 check the review round's fixes), and `m8-sd1-drum-names` for the D1 part
below (its own steps 1 to 13). About fifteen minutes for the first part and
twenty for the second, with headphones and any instrument on the track (the
built-in synth is enough
to hear that two sounds are two notes).

D0 ships the map's model, its playback and three commands; the editor that
mutes a sound and an icon column arrive with D1. Steps 4 and 7 need that
editor, so they wait for MT-DRUM-1's D1 part; everything else can be run now.

Build and start from the repository root in PowerShell:

```powershell
cargo build --release -p lumen-plugin-host
```

```powershell
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

1. Add a MIDI track (Ctrl+Shift+T) and draw a four-bar clip with a note on 36
   (C1) on every beat and a note on 38 (D1) on beats 2 and 4.
2. Right-click the track header, choose **Drum map…**, then **General MIDI**.
   **Expect:** the window closes, the track keeps its notes, and the notice
   says the map was attached. Press Space: the kick and the snare are two
   different sounds.
3. Right-click the header again, choose **Drum map…**, then **808 style**.
   **Expect:** the notes in the piano roll are exactly where they were (same
   bars, same pitches). A second map now sits in the project — chooser rows
   list both names.
4. Open the map's editor (right-click the header, **Drum map › Edit the drum
   map…**, or the palette's "Drum map…"). **Expect:** the window lists the
   kit's sounds; the kick's M mutes it (the kick is gone from playback at once
   and the snare plays on), and clicking it again brings it back. Each is one
   undo step.
5. Right-click the header, choose **Detach drum map**. **Expect:** the track
   keeps every note and plays them as written again.
6. Attach **General MIDI** once more. Select the clip, right-click it, choose
   **Convert drum map…**, keep **Keep the notes the target map lacks** ticked,
   and pick the 808-style map. **Expect:** each hit keeps its sound, in the
   other map's notes; the track now uses that map. Ctrl+Z once gives every note
   back, note for note.
7. *(D1)* Swap two maps whose sounds sit on different notes and convert with
   the option off: the notes whose sound the target lacks are gone, the rest
   moved. Undo gives them all back.
8. Save the project, close Lumen, reopen it. **Expect:** the maps and the
   track's choice come back, and playback sounds as it did.
9. **A locate into two hats** (the review's H1). With the 808-style map
   attached (its three hats choke each other), draw an open hat (46, Open Hat)
   held over beats 1 to 4 and a closed hat (42, Closed Hat) on beat 2. Press
   Space, then click the ruler between beats 2 and 3 — a locate into both,
   where both would sound — and press Space to stop. **Expect:** the closed
   hat sounds and ends with the transport, and nothing keeps ringing after
   Stop. With Loop on over that span, a wrap does the same.
10. **A mapped pad while recording** (M1). Arm the track, press F9 to punch
    in over the clip's kick, and play the pad's note 36 (C1). **Expect:** one
    kick under the take, not two.
11. **Capture MIDI on a mapped track** (M2). With the map attached, play the
    pad whose sound plays another note, then Shift+F9. **Expect:** the captured
    clip holds the note the pad sent (the map's input note), which plays the
    same sound.

## The map editor and the key column (D1's part of MT-DRUM-1)

**State:** Not run. Branch: `m8-sd1-drum-names`. About twenty minutes, with
headphones, the built-in synth, and a MIDI keyboard if one is at hand (only
step 6 needs it).

1. Add a MIDI track (Ctrl+Shift+T), draw a two-bar clip with a hit on each
   beat, and give the track **General MIDI** (right-click the header, **Drum
   map › Choose a drum map…**). **Expect:** the map editor opens on the
   chosen kit at once, with the sound rows, and the header's row count is 47.
2. Rename the first sound: click its name, type "Kick in", press **Enter**.
   **Expect:** the row's name changes, the piano roll's key column shows it at
   that note, and one **Undo** gives the old name back.
3. With the keyboard only: click the name once, press Enter (the cursor
   moves to the note), type a free note such as 90, press Enter. **Expect:**
   the sound moves to note 90 and the key column follows. Type a note another
   sound has: the window says another sound answers to it and the row keeps
   its note.
4. Click a row's icon. **Expect:** a 5 × 3 grid of the fifteen drawings, each
   named on hover, the current one lit. With the keyboard (Tab to the icon,
   Space), the arrows move the ring and Enter chooses; one Undo takes it back.
5. Mute a sound (its M), solo another (its S), set a channel, a choke group and
   a quantize and a length in their columns, and change the colour with the
   swatch. **Expect:** each is one undo step; the muted sound is silent and a
   soloed one plays alone; the key column dims a silent sound and shows M.
6. Press a row's **Learn** and play a note on a MIDI input. **Expect:** the
   sound moves to that note and a notice says so; one undo step.
7. Drag a row by its icon to another place, then Alt+Up/Alt+Down on a row's
   field. **Expect:** the map's order changes (the lanes' order in D2), one
   undo step each.
8. **Make unique** on a track that shares its map with another: both tracks
   read the same map first (attach one map to both), press Make unique on one.
   **Expect:** only that track gets a copy ("… copy") and edits to it stop
   reaching the other track.
9. **Save as user map…**, then **Import…** the file into another project
   (File › New, add a MIDI track, **Drum map › Import a drum map…**).
   **Expect:** the imported map is identical (names, notes, channels, colours,
   icons) and the track plays through it. A map of the same name saved twice is
   kept as two files, not replaced.
10. **Export…** to a folder, then hand-edit the file to break it (delete a
    line). Import it again. **Expect:** the window refuses it and says why in
    full ("… is not a drum map Lumen can read: …"), and nothing in the project
    changes.
11. Open a clip on the mapped track. **Expect:** the key column shows the
    kit's sound names and drawings at their notes, 176 pt wide, the unused
    keys dimmed. Press the **Keys** button at the editor's right (and run
    "Hide unmapped keys" from the palette): the roll folds to the kit's rows;
    notes stay where they were and play the same. Press it again.
12. Switch to the light theme, and to 150% and 200% scale (Preferences).
    Repeat 1, 4 and 11. **Expect:** nothing overlaps or is cut, the table's
    columns line up, and the notice wraps rather than running off.
13. Compare the window with `docs/specimens/m8/chosen.html` (the Lanes card's
    name column, 176 pt: colour bar, 16 pt drawing, name) and the Mappings
    window for the dialog language, and the key column with the same card.

## MT-ICONS-1 — The fifteen drum sound icons (D1, the icons)

**State:** Not run by hand: nothing in the interface draws them yet (D1's
map editor, its icon popover and D2's lanes come next). The look was checked in
the shots harness only. Branch: `m8-sd1-drum-names`. About five minutes, no
headphones needed.

**Now (the sheet):**

```powershell
New-Item -ItemType Directory -Force target\shots-d1-icons
$env:SHOTS = "$PWD\target\shots-d1-icons"
cargo test -p lumen-app --test shots drum_icons -- --ignored --test-threads=1
```

Then open the six files in `target\shots-d1-icons`: `drum-icons-dark-1`,
`-1.5`, `-2` and the same for `light`. Each shows the fifteen glyphs at 12,
16, 20 and 28 pt in the text colour, then every glyph at 16 pt in three
track colours.

**When the lanes and the map editor are reachable:**

1. Make an instrument track, attach the General MIDI map (D1), and create a
   MIDI clip with a kick, a snare and an open hi-hat.
2. Open the clip in the drum editor (D2). **Expect:** each lane starts with
   its sound's glyph in the lane colour or in `text_secondary`; the icons
   read as the pieces they name, at the lane height and at 28 pt on a tall
   track.
3. Open the icon column's popover (D1). **Expect:** a 5 x 3 grid of the
   fifteen glyphs; hovering or focusing a choice shows its name ("Closed
   hat"), Tab moves through them, Enter chooses one, and the lane's icon
   changes in one undo step.
4. Make a track per sound (D4's routing) and play. **Expect:** each track's
   header shows its sound's glyph in place of the colour chip, and it lights
   while that sound plays.
5. Switch to the light theme, and to 150% and 200% scale (Preferences).
   Repeat 2 and 3. **Expect:** nothing overlaps or is cut, and the glyphs
   stay crisp at each lane height.
6. Compare each glyph by eye with `docs/specimens/m8/round3-dark.png` and
   `round3-light.png` (the "Every icon" card). Each should read as the
   specimen does: a head-on kick; a snares-under-the-head snare; a hoop with
   a stick across it; two hands and sparks; the two hi-hats closed, with a
   pedal, and open with air between; a shell with lugs and the taller floor
   tom on legs; a cymbal on edge with its strike; a ride with its bell; a
   cowbell; a shaker with loose beads; a conga; a struck star for Effect.
7. Look at the smallest track height. **Expect:** the icons shrink with the
   row and never spill out of it.

## MT-FX-1 — Built-in effects: Prism, the utility device (B0; the browser and factory presets join in B6)

**State:** Not run. Branch: `m8-sb0-effect-path`. About ten minutes, with
headphones or speakers.

Build and start from the repository root in PowerShell:

```powershell
cargo build --release -p lumen-plugin-host
```

```powershell
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

1. Drag an audio file onto the arrangement. Select its track. Run **Add Prism
   (utility)** from the command palette (Ctrl+K, "Prism"). **Expect:** the
   Devices tab shows one card called **Prism** with **Utility** under it, a deep
   tinted picture of a beam split by a prism (the same in both themes, no
   "P"), eight controls (Gain, Pan, Width, Mono below, Polarity L, Polarity R,
   Swap L/R, DC block) and **All 8 parameters**. The footer says
   "audio → audio · 0.0 ms".
1b. Undo, then right-click the track's header and choose **Add Prism (utility)**.
   **Expect:** it lands on that track in one step. Right-click the empty room in
   the Devices tray (or past the last card) and choose it there too.
2. Play, and drag **Gain** down about half way. **Expect:** the track gets
   quieter smoothly, with no click; the knob reads the value in dB ("−18.6 dB"
   and so on); Ctrl+Z puts the gain and the sound back in one step.
3. Click the **Gain** value and type `-6`, then Enter. **Expect:** the knob
   reads "−6.0 dB" at once and Ctrl+Z undoes it. Type `loud` in the same place.
   **Expect:** a note saying Prism did not understand "loud", and nothing
   changes.
4. Turn **Width** to 0 %. **Expect:** the track goes mono. At 200 % the sides
   spread. Turn **Mono below** to about 120 Hz on a bass-heavy file. **Expect:**
   the low end centres, the rest is untouched. Switch **Polarity L** to
   Inverted, then **Swap L/R** on and off. **Expect:** no click at any of them.
5. Play, right-click the **Gain** knob, choose **Automate**. **Expect:** an
   automation lane appears under the track, reading "Prism · Gain". Draw a
   fade-out on it. **Expect:** the track follows, sample for sample (the level
   falls with the curve), and the knob follows the lane while it plays. Move
   the knob by hand: its ring turns amber and the chip says "Overridden"; the
   lane's **Re-enable** button (or Ctrl+Shift+R) gives it back.
6. Right-click the **Gain** knob, choose **Learn a controller…**, and move a
   knob on a MIDI controller. **Expect:** the next controller moves Prism's
   gain, as a hand on the knob would.
7. Ctrl+K, **All parameters**. **Expect:** the eight parameters with their
   own words, "All 8" and "On the strip 8", and a star on each. Take one off
   and put it back; the card follows.
8. On the card, click the preset field (**Preset of Prism**), then **Save
   as…**, name it "Bright" and press Enter. **Expect:** a
   `Bright.lumenpreset` file in `Documents\Lumen Presets\utility` (the
   device's stable name, so renaming Prism later keeps it), and a note
   saying it was saved. Change the gain, then choose **Bright** from the same
   menu. **Expect:** the saved gain comes back, and Ctrl+Z restores the one
   before it, in one step. A file someone edited to say `"version": 2` is
   refused with a sentence saying it came from a newer version.
9. Add a second audio track and copy the card to it (the card's menu, **Copy
   to track**). **Expect:** the copy sounds the same and its controls move
   independently.
10. While the song plays, bypass the card with its power button. **Expect:** no
    click; over about 20 ms the track is back at the file's own level, the
    footer says "bypassed", and the device stops using CPU. Turn it on again:
    the effect glides back in, with no click.
11. Save the project, close Lumen, open it again. **Expect:** the card, its
    values, its lane and its controller mapping are as they were, and the sound
    is the same.
12. Look at the card in the light theme, and at 150 % and 200 % UI scale.
    **Expect:** the grey picture and the name in both themes; nothing overlaps
    at the smallest window.

**What cannot be checked here:** the exact sound of the utility's mono fold and
DC block (by ear, on real material), and the dropouts counter's behaviour on a
busy project.

## MT-FX-2 — Built-in effects: Iris, the parametric EQ (B1; the compressor and the limiter join in B2)

**State:** Not run. Branch: `m8-sb1-eq`. About fifteen minutes, with headphones
or speakers, on material with a broad spectrum (a drum loop and a vocal are
better than a sine).

Build and start from the repository root in PowerShell:

```powershell
cargo build --release -p lumen-plugin-host
```

```powershell
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

1. Drag an audio file onto the arrangement and select its track. Run **Add Iris
   (EQ)** from the command palette (Ctrl+K, "Iris"), and again from the Devices
   tab's right-click menu. **Expect:** the Devices tab shows a card called
   **Iris** with **Parametric EQ** under it, a deep screen with the curve, a
   coloured point for each of eight bands, the labels 100, 1k and 10k, three
   lit dials (Freq, Gain, Q), eight numbered pills in two rows of four, and
   **All 38**. The card looks like the Iris of `docs/specimens/m8/round2-dark.png`.
2. On a fresh Iris, play the file and turn its power button off and on.
   **Expect:** no change in the sound either way (a fresh Iris is flat, to the
   last bit). While the file plays a soft coloured mist moves behind the curve.
3. Drag band 3's point (250 Hz, a yellow point on the curve) up about a fifth of
   the screen and a little to the right. **Expect:** the curve lifts at that
   band, the point wears the halo and blades, the chip over it reads its
   frequency and gain ("360 Hz +8.5"), the three dials follow it (Freq, Gain,
   Q in yellow), and the sound gains a broad bump there. One Ctrl+Z takes the
   whole drag back, frequency and gain together. Hold Shift while dragging: the
   point moves a tenth as far.
4. **Picking.** Click pill 5, then pill 2. **Expect:** the dials change to that
   band's values and colour each time and nothing is added to the undo history.
   Click a point: its pill lights. Tab to a point: its band is picked. Double-click
   a pill: the band turns off (its point turns hollow and dim) and on again.
   Double-click a point: its gain goes back to 0.0.
5. With a point focused (click it, or Tab to it), press Enter and type `1k` then
   Enter. **Expect:** the Freq dial's field opens over the dial, and the point
   moves to 1.00 kHz at once. A typed `loud` changes nothing. The wheel over a
   point is its Q; the arrow keys move it, Shift finer, Page Up and Down its Q.
6. **The low cut**: click pill 1, then drag its point right to about 120 Hz.
   Expect the bottom to thin out. The second dial now says **Slope** and the
   third has nothing to set (a dash); the wheel on the point steps the slope
   from 12 to 48 dB/oct. **A notch**: set band 5's Type to Notch in All
   parameters and pick it: its Gain dial goes dash, and its Q dial narrows the
   notch over a ringing part of the material.
7. **The analyser**: while the file plays, watch the mist behind the curve.
   **Expect:** it moves with the music, goes still when playback stops, and is
   off when the card is folded or the tab shows another track (no CPU spent on
   it). With **Reduce motion** on in Preferences the screen is still while
   playing. Set the **Analyzer** parameter to **Input** in All parameters: the
   mist stops following the EQ's own curve.
8. Right-click band 3's point, choose **Automate**, and draw a fade on the lane.
   **Expect:** the band's gain follows the lane sample for sample and the point
   follows it. Drag the point: its ring turns amber; **Re-enable** gives the
   lane back. Automate a band's **frequency** and sweep it across the material:
   **Expect:** no zipper noise and no clicks.
9. **Bypass** the device with its power button while it plays (with a strong
   curve set). **Expect:** no click; over about 20 ms the track is back to the
   file's own sound and the footer says "bypassed". Turn it on again: it glides
   back in.
10. Ctrl+K, **All parameters**. **Expect:** all 38 with their own words ("2
    Freq", "2.50 kHz"), a star on each, and eight on the strip. Take one off and
    put it back; the card follows.
11. Save the project, close Lumen and open it again. **Expect:** the curve, the
    bands, the output gain, the analyser's side and the lanes come back, and the
    sound is the same. A project saved with Iris must open: if the save says
    "a built-in device holds plugin-only data", that is a bug (it did, before
    B1 fixed the check).
12. **The steps.** Drag the detail panel's top edge down. **Expect:** the card
    steps to a short one (a smaller screen, the dials on its lower edge, eight
    small pills in a row under it) and then a compact one (a thin live curve
    with a dot for each band, the three dials under it with their values);
    a click on a dot picks its band. Fold the card: a rail with the name set on
    end.
13. Look at the card in the light theme, and at 150 % and 200 % UI scale.
    **Expect:** the deep screen is the same in both themes; nothing overlaps at
    the smallest window.

**What cannot be checked here:** how the EQ sounds on the owner's material (the
curve's shape is right; the "musical" settings are taste), the analyser's
readings against a real spectrum analyser, and the CPU cost on a large project
(the bench measures it: 7 µs an instance a 128-frame block).
