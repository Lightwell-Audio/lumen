# Drum maps, the drum editor and built-in devices: manual tests

## MT-DRUM-1 — Drum maps: attach, swap, detach and convert (D0; D1 extends it)

**State:** Not run. Branch: `m8-sd0-drum-map-model` (the review round's
fixes are on it; steps 9 to 12 are theirs). About fifteen minutes,
with headphones and any instrument on the track (the built-in synth is enough
to hear that two sounds are two notes).

D0 ships the map's model, its playback and three commands; the editor that
mutes a sound and an icon column arrive with D1. Steps 4 and 7 need that
editor, so they wait for MT-DRUM-1's D1 part; everything else can be run now.

Build and start from the repository root in PowerShell:

```powershell
cargo build --release -p lumen-plugin-host
```

```powershell
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

1. Add a MIDI track (Ctrl+Shift+T) and draw a four-bar clip with a note on 36
   (C1) on every beat and a note on 38 (D1) on beats 2 and 4.
2. Right-click the track header, choose **Drum map…**, then **General MIDI**.
   **Expect:** the window closes, the track keeps its notes, and the notice
   says the map was attached. Press Space: the kick and the snare are two
   different sounds.
3. Right-click the header again, choose **Drum map…**, then **808 style**.
   **Expect:** the notes in the piano roll are exactly where they were (same
   bars, same pitches). A second map now sits in the project — chooser rows
   list both names.
4. *(D1)* Open the map's editor and mute the kick's sound. **Expect:** the
   kick is gone from playback at once and the snare plays on. Unmute: it
   returns. Each is one undo step.
5. Right-click the header, choose **Detach drum map**. **Expect:** the track
   keeps every note and plays them as written again.
6. Attach **General MIDI** once more. Select the clip, right-click it, choose
   **Convert drum map…**, keep **Keep the notes the target map lacks** ticked,
   and pick the 808-style map. **Expect:** each hit keeps its sound, in the
   other map's notes; the track now uses that map. Ctrl+Z once gives every note
   back, note for note.
7. *(D1)* Swap two maps whose sounds sit on different notes and convert with
   the option off: the notes whose sound the target lacks are gone, the rest
   moved. Undo gives them all back.
8. Save the project, close Lumen, reopen it. **Expect:** the maps and the
   track's choice come back, and playback sounds as it did.
9. **A locate into two hats** (the review's H1). With the 808-style map
   attached (its three hats choke each other), draw an open hat (46, Open Hat)
   held over beats 1 to 4 and a closed hat (42, Closed Hat) on beat 2. Press
   Space, then click the ruler between beats 2 and 3 — a locate into both,
   where both would sound — and press Space to stop. **Expect:** the closed
   hat sounds and ends with the transport, and nothing keeps ringing after
   Stop. With Loop on over that span, a wrap does the same.
10. **A mapped pad while recording** (M1). Arm the track, press F9 to punch
    in over the clip's kick, and play the pad's note 36 (C1). **Expect:** one
    kick under the take, not two.
11. **Capture MIDI on a mapped track** (M2). With the map attached, play the
    pad whose sound plays another note, then Shift+F9. **Expect:** the captured
    clip holds the note the pad sent (the map's input note), which plays the
    same sound.
12. **Detach names its track** (L1). Select one mapped track, open **Drum
    map…**, click another track's header while the window stays open, then
    **Detach the drum map**. **Expect:** the track the window named loses its
    map, not the one clicked after.

## MT-FX-1 — Built-in effects: Prism, the utility device (B0; the browser and factory presets join in B6)

**State:** Not run. Branch: `m8-sb0-effect-path`. About ten minutes, with
headphones or speakers.

Build and start from the repository root in PowerShell:

```powershell
cargo build --release -p lumen-plugin-host
```

```powershell
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

1. Drag an audio file onto the arrangement. Select its track. Run **Add Prism
   (utility)** from the command palette (Ctrl+K, "Prism"). **Expect:** the
   Devices tab shows one card called **Prism** with **Utility** under it, a deep
   tinted picture of a beam split by a prism (the same in both themes, no
   "P"), eight controls (Gain, Pan, Width, Mono below, Polarity L, Polarity R,
   Swap L/R, DC block) and **All 8 parameters**. The footer says
   "audio → audio · 0.0 ms".
1b. Undo, then right-click the track's header and choose **Add Prism (utility)**.
   **Expect:** it lands on that track in one step. Right-click the empty room in
   the Devices tray (or past the last card) and choose it there too.
2. Play, and drag **Gain** down about half way. **Expect:** the track gets
   quieter smoothly, with no click; the knob reads the value in dB ("−18.6 dB"
   and so on); Ctrl+Z puts the gain and the sound back in one step.
3. Click the **Gain** value and type `-6`, then Enter. **Expect:** the knob
   reads "−6.0 dB" at once and Ctrl+Z undoes it. Type `loud` in the same place.
   **Expect:** a note saying Prism did not understand "loud", and nothing
   changes.
4. Turn **Width** to 0 %. **Expect:** the track goes mono. At 200 % the sides
   spread. Turn **Mono below** to about 120 Hz on a bass-heavy file. **Expect:**
   the low end centres, the rest is untouched. Switch **Polarity L** to
   Inverted, then **Swap L/R** on and off. **Expect:** no click at any of them.
5. Play, right-click the **Gain** knob, choose **Automate**. **Expect:** an
   automation lane appears under the track, reading "Prism · Gain". Draw a
   fade-out on it. **Expect:** the track follows, sample for sample (the level
   falls with the curve), and the knob follows the lane while it plays. Move
   the knob by hand: its ring turns amber and the chip says "Overridden"; the
   lane's **Re-enable** button (or Ctrl+Shift+R) gives it back.
6. Right-click the **Gain** knob, choose **Learn a controller…**, and move a
   knob on a MIDI controller. **Expect:** the next controller moves Prism's
   gain, as a hand on the knob would.
7. Ctrl+K, **All parameters**. **Expect:** the eight parameters with their
   own words, "All 8" and "On the strip 8", and a star on each. Take one off
   and put it back; the card follows.
8. On the card, click the preset field (**Preset of Prism**), then **Save
   as…**, name it "Bright" and press Enter. **Expect:** a
   `Bright.lumenpreset` file in `Documents\Lumen Presets\utility` (the
   device's stable name, so renaming Prism later keeps it), and a note
   saying it was saved. Change the gain, then choose **Bright** from the same
   menu. **Expect:** the saved gain comes back, and Ctrl+Z restores the one
   before it, in one step. A file someone edited to say `"version": 2` is
   refused with a sentence saying it came from a newer version.
9. Add a second audio track and copy the card to it (the card's menu, **Copy
   to track**). **Expect:** the copy sounds the same and its controls move
   independently.
10. While the song plays, bypass the card with its power button. **Expect:** no
    click; over about 20 ms the track is back at the file's own level, the
    footer says "bypassed", and the device stops using CPU. Turn it on again:
    the effect glides back in, with no click.
11. Save the project, close Lumen, open it again. **Expect:** the card, its
    values, its lane and its controller mapping are as they were, and the sound
    is the same.
12. Look at the card in the light theme, and at 150 % and 200 % UI scale.
    **Expect:** the grey picture and the name in both themes; nothing overlaps
    at the smallest window.

**What cannot be checked here:** the exact sound of the utility's mono fold and
DC block (by ear, on real material), and the dropouts counter's behaviour on a
busy project.

## MT-FX-3 — Gravity, the compressor, and Horizon, the limiter (B2)

**State:** Not run. Branch: `m8-sb2-dynamics` (its faces are placeholders until
the card's shared face is rebuilt). About fifteen minutes, with headphones and
a drum loop or a full mix.

Build and start from the repository root in PowerShell:

```powershell
cargo build --release -p lumen-plugin-host
```

```powershell
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

1. Drag an audio file onto the arrangement and select its track. Ctrl+K,
   **Add Gravity (compressor)**. **Expect:** the Devices tab shows one card
   called **Gravity** with **Compressor** under it, a deep violet screen with
   the transfer curve and a gain-reduction meter, four controls (Pull, Ratio,
   Attack, Release) and **All 10 parameters**. The footer says
   "audio → audio · 0.0 ms".
2. Play. Drag **Pull** down until the meter shows reduction. **Expect:** the
   track is pulled down smoothly on the loud parts and comes back; the meter's
   number and its face follow the sound, and the body on the curve sits where
   the signal is. Double-click Pull: it returns to −18 dB in one step, and
   Ctrl+Z takes the drag back in one step.
3. Turn **Makeup** (in All parameters) Off and on. **Expect:** the level jumps
   down and back with no click (it glides). Set **Knee** to 0 and back to 6 dB.
   **Expect:** the curve on the screen changes from a crease to a round knee,
   and the sound with it.
4. Right-click **Ratio** and choose **Automate**; draw a rise from 2:1 to
   10:1. **Expect:** the track's level follows the curve, sample for sample,
   and the knob follows the lane while it plays.
5. **Key it from a kick.** Add a second track with a drum loop. On Gravity's
   card, click its **Sidechain** chooser and choose the drum track (Post FX).
   **Expect:** the row reads "Sidechain source, Drums, Post FX", its light
   follows the kick, and the track ducks in time with the kick — on the sample
   the kick plays (the meter is compensation-aligned). Turn **Listen** on: the
   key itself is heard, with the key's high-pass applied. Ctrl+Z once removes
   the sidechain.
6. Ctrl+K, **Add Horizon (limiter)**. **Expect:** a card called **Horizon**
   with **Limiter** under it, a sunset screen whose ceiling is drawn as a
   horizon line with the ceiling's value, a gain-reduction meter, and four
   controls (Ceiling, Release, Look, Detect). The footer says
   "audio → audio · 1.5 ms" and the transport ends with "1.5 ms compensation".
7. Play something loud. **Expect:** nothing clips: the meter shows reduction
   and no sample passes the ceiling. Watch the CPU/Dropouts readout for
   dropouts during a minute of playback.
8. Drag **Ceiling** down to −12 dB. **Expect:** the horizon line moves down,
   the track gets quieter and never overshoots; the compensation chip still
   reads the same number (the ceiling is not a latency).
9. Set **Look** to 10 ms. **Expect:** the footer and the transport's
   compensation chip read "10.0 ms", and after a moment the sound is unchanged
   in level but the limiter catches its peaks earlier (a gap or a repeat of a
   few milliseconds is heard as the routing re-times — that is the documented
   behaviour of a latency change while playing). Set it back to 1.5 ms.
10. Switch **Detect** to True. **Expect:** the peaks between the samples are
    caught too (turn the ceiling down to −3 dB on a bright, distorted loop and
    listen for a hard, flat top either way, and compare against a sample-peak
    meter on the master: the true-peak reading never passes the ceiling by
    more than 0.2 dB).
11. Bypass each with its power button while playing. **Expect:** no click; the
    dry signal returns over about 20 ms and the footer says "bypassed".
12. Save, close Lumen, reopen. **Expect:** both cards, their values and
    Gravity's sidechain come back, and the mix sounds as it did. Look at both
    in the light theme and at 150 % and 200 % UI scale; nothing overlaps at the
    smallest window.
13. **All parameters and presets.** Ctrl+K, **All parameters**: Gravity lists
    ten, Horizon four, in their own words. Save a preset from each card and
    load it back (one undo step). Copy each card to another track and check the
    copy sounds the same.

**What cannot be checked here:** the character of the compressor and the
limiter's sound by ear on real material (whether 4:1 at −18 dB with automatic
make-up is the right default), and how it behaves on a project with a hundred
instances (the bench numbers are in the ADR).
