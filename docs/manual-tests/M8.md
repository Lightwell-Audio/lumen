# Drum maps, the drum editor and built-in devices: manual tests

Checks for M8's drum half and device half, added as each becomes reachable
(`docs/milestones/M8.md`).

## MT-DRUM-1 — Drum maps: attach, swap, detach and convert (D0; D1 extends it)

**State:** Not run. Branch: `m8-sd0-drum-map-model`. About fifteen minutes,
with headphones and any instrument on the track (the built-in synth is enough
to hear that two sounds are two notes).

D0 ships the map's model, its playback and three commands; the editor that
mutes a sound and an icon column arrive with D1. Steps 4 and 7 need that
editor, so they wait for MT-DRUM-1's D1 part; everything else can be run now.

Build and start from the repository root in PowerShell:

```powershell
cargo build --release -p lumen-plugin-host
```

```powershell
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

1. Add a MIDI track (Ctrl+Shift+T) and draw a four-bar clip with a note on 36
   (C1) on every beat and a note on 38 (D1) on beats 2 and 4.
2. Right-click the track header, choose **Drum map…**, then **General MIDI**.
   **Expect:** the window closes, the track keeps its notes, and the notice
   says the map was attached. Press Space: the kick and the snare are two
   different sounds.
3. Right-click the header again, choose **Drum map…**, then **808 style**.
   **Expect:** the notes in the piano roll are exactly where they were (same
   bars, same pitches). A second map now sits in the project — chooser rows
   list both names.
4. *(D1)* Open the map's editor and mute the kick's sound. **Expect:** the
   kick is gone from playback at once and the snare plays on. Unmute: it
   returns. Each is one undo step.
5. Right-click the header, choose **Detach drum map**. **Expect:** the track
   keeps every note and plays them as written again.
6. Attach **General MIDI** once more. Select the clip, right-click it, choose
   **Convert drum map…**, keep **Keep the notes the target map lacks** ticked,
   and pick the 808-style map. **Expect:** each hit keeps its sound, in the
   other map's notes; the track now uses that map. Ctrl+Z once gives every note
   back, note for note.
7. *(D1)* Swap two maps whose sounds sit on different notes and convert with
   the option off: the notes whose sound the target lacks are gone, the rest
   moved. Undo gives them all back.
8. Save the project, close Lumen, reopen it. **Expect:** the maps and the
   track's choice come back, and playback sounds as it did.

## MT-ICONS-1 — The fifteen drum sound icons (D1, the icons)

**State:** Not run by hand: nothing in the interface draws them yet (D1's
map editor, its icon popover and D2's lanes come next). The look was checked in
the shots harness only. Branch: `m8-sd1-drum-names`. About five minutes, no
headphones needed.

**Now (the sheet):**

```powershell
New-Item -ItemType Directory -Force target\shots-d1-icons
$env:SHOTS = "$PWD\target\shots-d1-icons"
cargo test -p lumen-app --test shots drum_icons -- --ignored --test-threads=1
```

Then open the six files in `target\shots-d1-icons`: `drum-icons-dark-1`,
`-1.5`, `-2` and the same for `light`. Each shows the fifteen glyphs at 12,
16, 20 and 28 pt in the text colour, then every glyph at 16 pt in three
track colours.

**When the lanes and the map editor are reachable:**

1. Make an instrument track, attach the General MIDI map (D1), and create a
   MIDI clip with a kick, a snare and an open hi-hat.
2. Open the clip in the drum editor (D2). **Expect:** each lane starts with
   its sound's glyph in the lane colour or in `text_secondary`; the icons
   read as the pieces they name, at the lane height and at 28 pt on a tall
   track.
3. Open the icon column's popover (D1). **Expect:** a 5 x 3 grid of the
   fifteen glyphs; hovering or focusing a choice shows its name ("Closed
   hat"), Tab moves through them, Enter chooses one, and the lane's icon
   changes in one undo step.
4. Make a track per sound (D4's routing) and play. **Expect:** each track's
   header shows its sound's glyph in place of the colour chip, and it lights
   while that sound plays.
5. Switch to the light theme, and to 150% and 200% scale (Preferences).
   Repeat 2 and 3. **Expect:** nothing overlaps or is cut, and the glyphs
   stay crisp at each lane height.
6. Compare each glyph by eye with `docs/specimens/m8/round3-dark.png` and
   `round3-light.png` (the "Every icon" card). Each should read as the
   specimen does: a head-on kick; a snares-under-the-head snare; a hoop with
   a stick across it; two hands and sparks; the two hi-hats closed, with a
   pedal, and open with air between; a shell with lugs and the taller floor
   tom on legs; a cymbal on edge with its strike; a ride with its bell; a
   cowbell; a shaker with loose beads; a conga; a struck star for Effect.
7. Look at the smallest track height. **Expect:** the icons shrink with the
   row and never spill out of it.
