## MT-CLIP-3 — The audio editor's range, envelopes and loop brace (S2b)

**Needs:** Lumen started as in `docs/DEVELOPMENT.md`, a project with an
audio clip of a few bars, and your ears (the Focusrite or any output).

1. Double-click the clip to open it in the Clip tab. Drag across its wave.
   *A range is picked in the accent colour, snapped to the grid; the info
   line says what the range does.* Hold Alt and drag: *the edges land
   anywhere.*
2. With the range picked, press Ctrl+E. *The clip is cut at both edges, in one
   step; Ctrl+Z puts it back.* Pick it again and press Delete, then Ctrl+Z.
3. Click the wave once. *A thin edit cursor appears, apart from the
   playhead.* Press Ctrl+E. *The clip is cut at the cursor.*
4. Turn Zero crossings on. Drag a range's edge over a loud part of the sound
   and play across the cut. *No click at the seam.* Turn it off and cut the
   same place for comparison.
5. Click **Gain**. *An envelope line shows; Pan is not lit.* Click the line
   three times. *Three points; Ctrl+Z removes the last.* Play. *The clip's
   volume follows the line.* Click **Pan** and repeat: *the pan follows.*
6. Press B with the pointer over the wave. *The pencil is on, and Gain lights
   if nothing was lit.* Drag across the wave. *A line of points is drawn and
   thinned out; one Ctrl+Z takes the whole stroke.* Press B again: *the
   pointer is back.*
7. Show an automation lane for the same track on the arrangement and pick a
   point there. *Picking points in the editor does not change which are
   picked on the arrangement.*
8. Click the band under the editor's ruler. *The clip loops; a brace shows its
   two ends.* Play. *What plays up to the first repeat is unchanged, then it
   repeats.* Drag the brace's end a beat earlier. *The loop gets shorter, in one
   step.* Drag the brace end past the editor's edge. *The view scrolls along.*
9. Change the tempo, with the envelope shown. *Each point stays on the same
   sound.*
10. With the keyboard only: F6 to the detail panel, Tab to the wave, Right
    to place the cursor, Ctrl+E to cut; Shift+End to pick a range, Delete to
    take it out; Tab to the envelope, Enter to add a point, Ctrl+Right to pick
    it, Up and Down to change it; Tab to the loop, Enter to loop it.
    *Every step works and each is one undo step.*
11. Repeat steps 1, 5 and 8 in the light theme and at 150% and 200% UI
    scale. *Nothing overlaps or is cut, except as MT-CLIP-2's step 10 already
    records for the smallest window at 200%.*

## MT-WARP-1 — Melodic and Full mix by ear, and clips that follow the tempo (S4c, S4d)

   and Never, with Loops chosen.*
2. Name a loop file `Drums 100 BPM.wav` and drop it on an empty project at 120
   BPM. *It comes in as a clip as long in beats as the file is at 100 BPM and
   starts on a bar line; its wave is drawn across those beats.* Play it. *It
   plays faster than the file, at the same pitch, and stays on the beat for
   several bars against the metronome.* Drop `Drums.wav` (no BPM in its name).
   *It plays at its own speed, as it always did.*
3. Change the song's tempo (Tempo lane or the transport) to 90, then 140, while
   the first clip loops. *The clip follows each change, in time with the
   metronome. Listen for: warbling or a ringing, smeared transients on the drums,
   and the pitch staying put. Say which tempos sound worst.* Set 100 again (the
   tempo the file was made at). *The clip sounds as the file does at its own
   tempo (no processing).* Set 120 for the next steps.
4. Arm a track, record two bars with the metronome at 120, stop. Change the
   tempo to 100. *The take follows: it plays slower and stays on the bars, at
   its own pitch.* Undo the tempo change. *The take plays as it was recorded.*
5. Record again while a tempo ramp from 100 to 140 plays under the take
   (draw it in the tempo lane first). *The take plays back as it was played,
   in the same ramp, with no drift against the metronome by the end.*
6. Preferences › Editing › Follow tempo › Never; record or drop a file. *The
   new clip plays at its own speed and the tempo changes leave it alone.* Set
   Always: *a file with no BPM also follows, made at the song's tempo.*
7. With 20 or more such clips on separate tracks, change the tempo while
   playing and watch the CPU meter. *The audio does not drop out; a clip
   whose audio is not ready yet is held up to 2 seconds, then plays.* Say how
   many clips it took before you heard a problem.
8. Export the song with these clips (Full mix). *The file sounds the same as
   live playback.*

## MT-WARP-2 — Detection and the Warp chip on real loops (S5a)

**Needs:** Lumen started as in `docs/DEVELOPMENT.md` (`cargo build --release
-p lumen-plugin-host` first), headphones, "Play audio from disk" on (the
default), and four files you know the tempo of: a drum loop of whole bars
(say 96 BPM, with no tempo in its name), a loop with a swung or syncopated
feel, a sustained pad with no beat, and a long song (three minutes or more).

1. Set Preferences › Editing › New audio clips to *Never follow the tempo*
   (step 11 tests the default). Drop the drum loop on an empty project at
   120 BPM and double-click it.
   *The audio editor opens; a "Warp" chip stands left of the file's name.
   Faint accent lines mark where sounds start on the wave.* Open the chip.
   *It says "Listening to the file…" with a percentage for a moment, then
   "Detected 96.0 BPM" (the loop's true tempo to a tenth), the Sensitivity
   control and a count of transients.*
2. Turn on Warp clip. *The chip reads "Warp 96.0" in the accent; play the
   clip at 120 BPM: it plays faster and stays on the beat for eight bars
   against the metronome.* Undo (Ctrl+Z): *the clip plays as the file does.*
3. Drag Sensitivity from 0 to 100. *The lines on the wave increase in number
   and the count with them; the loudest hits are the first to appear. The
   file is not read again (no percentage).*
4. With the loop warped, press ×2, then ÷2. *The tempo doubles, then returns;
   each is one undo step. Say if the clip plays at half speed after ×2 and at
   double speed after ÷2 (it should: the file is now taken as made at twice
   the tempo, so the song stretches it to twice the length).* Type 90 into
   the tempo field and press Enter. *It becomes 90.0 and is one undo step.*
   Double-click the field: *it returns to 120.*
5. Press Set project tempo from clip. *The song's tempo (transport) becomes
   the clip's tempo, and the clip plays at the file's own speed. Undo
   restores 120.* Freeze the track (Frozen pill) and try again: *a notice
   says tempo changes wait for unfreezing.*
6. Put the edit cursor (click the wave) on the first clearly audible hit
   that is not on the downbeat, and press Warp from here. *That hit now sits
   on the nearest bar line of the arrangement and the loop stays in time
   after it. Undo restores it in one step.* Repeat with no cursor and the
   playhead on a hit.
7. Open the swung loop and the pad. *The swung loop gives a tempo or "not
   sure"; the pad says "No clear tempo found" and still shows transients if
   it has any. Say which tempos were wrong by a factor of two (use ×2 or
   ÷2 to correct them).*
8. Open the long song. *The chip shows progress and the UI stays responsive
   (scroll, zoom) while it reads; when it ends the tempo is offered. Open it
   again after restarting Lumen: the answer is immediate (it is in the
   cache).* Preferences › Files › Clear the cache, reopen: *it reads again.*
9. Lock the clip (Lock in the strip). *The Warp controls in the popover are
   disabled with the reason; Set project tempo and Use stay unavailable.*
