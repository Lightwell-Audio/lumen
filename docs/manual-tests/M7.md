### MT-AUTO-1 · Hearing lanes (needs a human and ears)
Slice A0 of M7 (ADR-0081). **Status: passed (owner, 2026-10-19; reported as
passed, with no step detail, device, driver or buffer size recorded).** There is no lane
interface until slice A1, so nothing on screen shows a lane: the project is made
by code and the test is by ear. In PowerShell, from the repository root:

```powershell
cargo run --release -p lumen-app -- tests\projects\automation-demo.lumen
```

(If Lumen opens empty, use **Open…** and choose
`tests\projects\automation-demo.lumen`.) The project is 120 BPM, eight bars, three
MIDI tracks of the MIDI demo: **Chords** (Poly), **Pad** (Pad) and **Bass**
(Bass). Each has one lane: the Pad's fader, the Chords' pan and the Bass's mute.
Use the audio device you normally use, at 128 frames if you can.

1. **The Pad swells.** Press Space. The pad starts almost silent and swells in
   over the first two bars to its full level, holds, and falls a little over the
   last two bars (18 dB by the end). Do you hear steps or zipper noise in the
   swell?
2. **The Chords sweep.** The chords move from the left speaker to the right over
   bars 1 and 2, back over 3 and 4, and again over 5 to 8. They do not jump, and
   hard left and hard right are fully one side.
3. **The Bass drops out.** The bass plays in bars 1 and 2, is silent in bar 3,
   plays in bars 4 to 6, is silent in bar 7 and plays in bar 8. Each drop and
   return is clean: no click, and it begins on the bar line.
4. **Jump.** While playing, click the ruler somewhere else a few times, including
   inside bar 3 and bar 7. The sound at the new place is what the lanes say there
   (a muted bass stays muted, the pad is at its level for that bar), with no
   click other than the usual 2 ms crossfade.
5. **Loop.** Click the loop brace above bars 1 to 4 to turn the loop on, and play.
   At each wrap the lanes read bar 1 again: the pad is near silent and swells in
   again, the chords start at the left, the bass plays. No click at the wrap other
   than the usual 2 ms crossfade.
6. **Tempo.** While playing, change the tempo in the transport bar (try 90 and
   160). The sweep, the swell and the drops stay on the same bars, and nothing
   clicks as the tempo moves.
7. **Edit nothing, hear the same.** Stop, save the project as a copy, close
   Lumen, open the copy and play: the same as step 1 to 3.
8. **Small buffers.** Set the buffer to 64 frames and play the whole project once:
   no dropout is counted, and the CPU reading is no higher than with the lanes
   removed (open `automation-demo.lumen` in a text editor, delete the three
   `"automation"` lists, save under another name and compare).

Record what you hear, the device, the driver and the buffer size, and anything
that differs from the above.

### MT-AUTO-2 · Seeing and adding a lane (needs a human)
Slice A1 of M7 (ADR-0082). **Status: passed (owner, 2026-10-19; reported as passed, no step detail), except step 12: the release frame benchmark could not give numbers, because `--stress` with the owner's audio device showed 100 "Samples/Take N.wav" load failures (KNOWN_ISSUES, 2026-10-19). Numbers are still due.** In PowerShell, from the repository
root:

```powershell
cargo run --release -p lumen-app -- tests\projects\automation-demo.lumen
```

1. **See.** Click the Pad track's name to select it and press **A**. The lane opens over its
   clips: the clips fade back, a blue line runs with its points (hollow circles), a chip at
   the top-left says "Volume". The track grows if it was short. Press A again: it goes.
2. **The button.** Unfold the Chords track (its arrow). Its second row ends with an **A**
   button; click it: lit while the lane shows. Hover it: the tip says what it does.
3. **Add.** Click an empty spot of the lane: a point appears on the beat nearest, at the
   height you clicked, and stays picked (solid, ringed). The info line at the bottom says
   what the pointer is over. Hold **Alt** and click: the point lands off the grid.
4. **Move.** Drag a point: it follows in time and height, snaps to the beat, never passes
   its neighbour. Release, press **Ctrl+Z**: it goes back in one step (not many small ones).
5. **Press and drag.** Press empty lane and drag: a point is added and moves with you; one
   Ctrl+Z removes it.
6. **Delete.** Click a point, press **Delete**; click the last one and Delete: the lane is
   gone and the fader is its own again. Ctrl+Z brings it back.
7. **Hear it.** Make a volume lane on a track with sound: a point at 0 dB on bar 1 and one at
   −30 dB on bar 3. Play: the track fades out. Drag the second point while it plays: the
   sound follows.
8. **Under the lane.** With a lane shown, **Ctrl+click** a clip: the clip is selected, and the
   lane gets no point. A plain click on the clip's place adds a point instead.
9. **Choose.** Click the chip: a list of Volume, Pan, Trim, Mute and the track's sends, each
   with its point count. Choose Pan, click: a pan lane. The Volume lane is untouched.
   Turn the lane off in the same menu: the chip says "off" and the sound plays unautomated.
10. **Keys only.** Without the mouse: select a track (arrow keys), **A**, move the playhead,
    then Ctrl+K "automation point" (Enter): a point at the playhead holding the control's
    value. Arrows move it, Delete removes it. Ctrl+K "automation parameter" opens the chooser.
11. **Looks.** At 100%, 150% and 200% scale, in the dark and the light theme: the chip, the
    points and the line are crisp, and nothing overlaps at the smallest window (960 x 540).
12. **Frame time (release).** With the machine idle:
    ```powershell
    cargo run --release -p lumen-app -- --stress --bench-lanes --bench-frames lanes.json
    cargo run --release -p lumen-app -- --stress --bench-frames plain.json
    ```
    Record both reports' scroll and zoom p99 (60 fps is 16.7 ms) and the idle repaints.

Record what you see, the scale and theme, and the two benchmark reports.
