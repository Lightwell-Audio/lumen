### MT-AUTO-1 · Hearing lanes (needs a human and ears)
Slice A0 of M7 (ADR-0081). **Status: passed (owner, 2026-10-19; reported as
passed, with no step detail, device, driver or buffer size recorded).** There is no lane
interface until slice A1, so nothing on screen shows a lane: the project is made
by code and the test is by ear. In PowerShell, from the repository root:

```powershell
cargo run --release -p lumen-app -- tests\projects\automation-demo.lumen
```

(If Lumen opens empty, use **Open…** and choose
`tests\projects\automation-demo.lumen`.) The project is 120 BPM, eight bars, three
MIDI tracks of the MIDI demo: **Chords** (Poly), **Pad** (Pad) and **Bass**
(Bass). Each has one lane: the Pad's fader, the Chords' pan and the Bass's mute.
Use the audio device you normally use, at 128 frames if you can.

1. **The Pad swells.** Press Space. The pad starts almost silent and swells in
   over the first two bars to its full level, holds, and falls a little over the
   last two bars (18 dB by the end). Do you hear steps or zipper noise in the
   swell?
2. **The Chords sweep.** The chords move from the left speaker to the right over
   bars 1 and 2, back over 3 and 4, and again over 5 to 8. They do not jump, and
   hard left and hard right are fully one side.
3. **The Bass drops out.** The bass plays in bars 1 and 2, is silent in bar 3,
   plays in bars 4 to 6, is silent in bar 7 and plays in bar 8. Each drop and
   return is clean: no click, and it begins on the bar line.
4. **Jump.** While playing, click the ruler somewhere else a few times, including
   inside bar 3 and bar 7. The sound at the new place is what the lanes say there
   (a muted bass stays muted, the pad is at its level for that bar), with no
   click other than the usual 2 ms crossfade.
5. **Loop.** Click the loop brace above bars 1 to 4 to turn the loop on, and play.
   At each wrap the lanes read bar 1 again: the pad is near silent and swells in
   again, the chords start at the left, the bass plays. No click at the wrap other
   than the usual 2 ms crossfade.
6. **Tempo.** While playing, change the tempo in the transport bar (try 90 and
   160). The sweep, the swell and the drops stay on the same bars, and nothing
   clicks as the tempo moves.
7. **Edit nothing, hear the same.** Stop, save the project as a copy, close
   Lumen, open the copy and play: the same as step 1 to 3.
8. **Small buffers.** Set the buffer to 64 frames and play the whole project once:
   no dropout is counted, and the CPU reading is no higher than with the lanes
   removed (open `automation-demo.lumen` in a text editor, delete the three
   `"automation"` lists, save under another name and compare).

Record what you hear, the device, the driver and the buffer size, and anything
that differs from the above.

### MT-AUTO-2 · Seeing and adding a lane (needs a human)

root:


1. **See.** Click the Pad track's name to select it and press **A**. The lane opens over its
   clips: the clips fade back, a blue line runs with its points (hollow circles), a chip at
   the top-left says "Volume". The track grows if it was short. Press A again: it goes.
2. **The button.** Unfold the Chords track (its arrow). Its second row ends with an **A**
   button; click it: lit while the lane shows. Hover it: the tip says what it does.
3. **Add.** Click an empty spot of the lane: a point appears on the beat nearest, at the
   height you clicked, and stays picked (solid, ringed). The info line at the bottom says
   what the pointer is over. Hold **Alt** and click: the point lands off the grid.
4. **Move.** Drag a point: it follows in time and height, snaps to the beat, never passes
   its neighbour. Release, press **Ctrl+Z**: it goes back in one step (not many small ones).
5. **Press and drag.** Press empty lane and drag: a point is added and moves with you; one
   Ctrl+Z removes it.
6. **Delete.** Click a point, press **Delete**; click the last one and Delete: the lane is

7. **Hear it.** Make a volume lane on a track with sound: a point at 0 dB on bar 1 and one at

8. **Under the lane.** With a lane shown, **Ctrl+click** a clip: the clip is selected, and the
   lane gets no point. A plain click on the clip's place adds a point instead.
9. **Choose.** Click the chip: a list of Volume, Pan, Trim, Mute and the track's sends, each
   with its point count. Choose Pan, click: a pan lane. The Volume lane is untouched.
   Turn the lane off in the same menu: the chip says "off" and the sound plays unautomated.
10. **Keys only.** Without the mouse: select a track (arrow keys), **A**, move the playhead,
    then Ctrl+K "automation point" (Enter): a point at the playhead holding the control's
    value. Arrows move it, Delete removes it. Ctrl+K "automation parameter" opens the chooser.
11. **Looks.** At 100%, 150% and 200% scale, in the dark and the light theme: the chip, the
    points and the line are crisp, and nothing overlaps at the smallest window (960 x 540).
12. **Frame time (release).** With the machine idle:
    ```powershell

    cargo run --release -p lumen-app -- --stress --bench-frames plain.json
    ```
    Record both reports' scroll and zoom p99 (60 fps is 16.7 ms) and the idle repaints.

### MT-AUTO-3 · Editing a lane (needs a human)
Slice A2 of M7 (ADR-0083). **Status: passed (owner, 2026-10-20; reported as passed, no step detail).** The owner's one note: copying and pasting *ranges* of time would make more sense than copying points, because the range to copy may have no points at its edges. To revisit after the core product is finished (KNOWN_ISSUES). In PowerShell, from the
repository root:

```powershell
cargo run --release -p lumen-app -- tests\projects\automation-demo.lumen
```

Select the Pad track, press **A**, and use a volume lane with about six points (click to
add them along the lane). Steps 12 and 13 are about looks and gestures that were built
without specimens: say what you would change.

1. **Box.** Hold **Shift** and drag a box on empty lane around two or three points: they
   turn solid with a ring, and a soft box follows the pointer. Drag a box around nothing: the
   picks are let go.
2. **Shift click.** Click a point, then **Shift+click** another: both are picked. Shift+click
   one of them again: it is let go.
3. **Move together.** With several picked, drag one of them: they all move by the same time
   and the same height, snap to the beat, and stop against a point that is not picked.
   A chip beside the point says the value and the bar.beat.sixteenth. **Ctrl+Z**: all of them
   return in one step.
4. **Resting places.** Drag a point slowly across 0 dB: it catches on 0 dB (a faint line
   across the lane) and again at the top and the bottom. Hold **Alt**: it moves freely, and
   the beat snap is off too. On a pan lane it catches at the centre.
5. **Keys on several.** With several picked: the arrows move them all (Left and Right a grid
   step, Up and Down a little), **Delete** takes them all, **Ctrl+Z** brings them back.
6. **Copy and paste.** Click in the lane, **Ctrl+A** (every point is picked), **Ctrl+C**,
   move the playhead to a later bar and **Ctrl+V**: the same points start at the playhead,
   and are picked. Pasting over points replaces the ones in its span. **Ctrl+Z** undoes the
   paste whole. Copy volume points, switch the chip to Pan and paste: Lumen says they are for
   another kind of parameter.
7. **Duplicate.** Pick a run of points and press **Ctrl+D**: the run repeats right after
   itself. Try one that starts and ends at the same level (it joins up) and one that
   rises (a saw: a drop, then the rise again).
8. **Segment shapes.** Pick one point. Its segment has a solid dot in the middle: drag it to
   bend the line (slow then quick, or the other way; hold **Alt** and drag for an S). In the
   palette (**Ctrl+K**) run "Make the automation segments steps", "...curved" and
   "...straight" on one or more picked points. Hear a volume lane with a step and a curve.
9. **The pencil.** Press **B** (or click Pencil in the strip beside the chip) and drag a
   wavy line across the lane: a smooth line follows the pointer and, afterwards, holds far
   fewer points than you drew (no more than needed to look the same). Drag back over what
   you drew: it is taken back. **Ctrl+Z** removes the whole stroke. **B** again returns to
   the pointer.
10. **The shapes.** Click Shape in the strip, then the chip beside it, and try Ramp, Sine,
    Triangle, Saw and Square: drag across four bars from a low height to a high one. The
    shape starts at the height you pressed at, swings between the two heights with one
    cycle to a grid step (change the grid and draw again) and ends where you let go. The
    saw drops at the end of each cycle; the square is steps. Each is one undo step. Hear
    a sine on the Pad's volume (a tremolo) and a square on its mute.
11. **Keys only.** Without the mouse: **A**, then Ctrl+K "Automation tool: sine" (Enter) and
    "Make the automation segments straight" work; the tool strip's buttons have the names
    "Automation pointer tool of ...", "...pencil tool..." and "...shape tool...".
12. **Looks.** At 100%, 150% and 200%, in both themes: the strip, the shape chip, the box,
    the bend dot (solid, apart from the hollow points) and the value chip are crisp and
    nothing overlaps at the smallest window (960 x 540). Is the strip next to the chip where
    you would look for it? Is the bend dot easy to tell from a picked point?
13. **Gestures.** Shift to box and a plain drag that adds a point is A1's behaviour kept. Would
    you rather a plain drag on empty lane drew the box (and the pencil added points while
    dragging)? Do the resting places catch too much or too little?

Record what you see, the scale and theme, and what you would change.


### MT-AUTO-4 · A lane on a plugin's parameter (needs a human, ears and real plugins)
Slice A3 of M7 (ADR-0084). **Status: passed (owner, 2026-10-20; reported as passed, no step detail, plugins, device or buffer size recorded). Re-run after the two fixes found in it (ADR-0084 amendments: the plugin host's stale queued value, and a plugin instrument offered a removed lane's last block): passed (owner, 2026-10-20; a lane switched off or removed, then the parameter changed, on an effect and an instrument).** The cloud runs covered the
test plugin only; this is the first time a real plugin's parameter is driven by a lane. Label
each plugin you use with an anonymous id (RP-n) in the record, as the earlier plugin runs do.
There is no host protocol change, but build the host program as usual:

```powershell
cargo build --release -p lumen-plugin-host
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

Use a project with a track that makes a steady sound (a held note on a plugin or built-in
instrument, or a loop), and one plugin **effect** whose parameter you can hear (a filter's
cutoff, a gain, a mix control; RP-a). Press **A** on the track.

1. **Find it.** Click the chip (it says "Volume"). The menu ends with "Parameters of
   *the effect's name*…": click it. A window lists the plugin's parameters with a search
   field; the field has the keyboard. Type part of a name ("cut"): the list narrows as you
   type. **Arrow keys and Enter** choose the highlighted one. Choose the cutoff (or your
   parameter): the window closes and the chip names it in the plugin's words, "Effect · Cutoff".
2. **Draw it.** Click along the lane to make four or five points (high, low, high). Press Space:
   the parameter moves with the lane, on the beats, and you hear the filter open and close
   (or the gain rise and fall). Is the movement smooth? Any zipper noise or stepping on a
   straight segment? (Plugins that smooth their own parameters differ: say which.)
3. **Steps and curves.** Pick two points and run "Make the automation segments steps" from
   **Ctrl+K**: the parameter jumps at the next point, on the beat. Run "…curved": it bends.
4. **Jump and loop.** While playing, click the ruler to jump inside a segment: the sound at the
   new place is what the lane says there. Turn the loop on over the lane and play: it
   repeats without a glitch at the wrap other than the usual 2 ms crossfade.
5. **The plugin's own window.** Open the effect's window while the lane plays and move the
   same parameter by hand. The lane's value comes back at once (this is expected until the
   next slice, which adds the override). Stop, and move it by hand: it moves and stays, and
   the lane takes it again when you play. Now switch the lane off from the chip's menu and
   move it: it stays where you put it.
6. **Instrument.** On a MIDI track with a plugin **instrument** (RP-b), choose one of its
   parameters the same way (a filter or an envelope time) and draw a lane. Play a held note:
   the lane moves the parameter, and a note played after the lane's change sounds as the
   lane says.
7. **Many parameters.** Open the chooser of the plugin that lists the most parameters you
   have (hundreds or thousands). Type quickly: the list follows without a pause, only a screen
   of rows is drawn, and the count at the bottom ("12 of 2,031") changes. Scroll the whole list.
8. **Two plugins.** On a track with two plugins, the window shows a pill for each. Switch
   between them: the list changes and the search is cleared.
9. **Save, undo, move.** Save, close, open: the lane is where you left it and plays. Undo a
   point edit: it comes back in one step. Drag the effect's card to another track (or
   Alt-drag to copy it): its lane goes with it (the lane appears on the other track; a copy
   brings its own). Delete the card: the lane goes; Ctrl+Z brings both back.
10. **Missing plugin.** Rename the plugin's file so Lumen cannot find it, restart, open the
    project: the lane is kept (the chip says "Effect · parameter 9" in place of the name) and
    nothing is lost in a save.
11. **Small buffers.** Set the buffer to 64 frames and play with ten lanes on one plugin: no
    dropout is counted, and the CPU reading is no higher than with the lanes switched off by
    more than a few points.
12. **Looks.** At 100%, 150% and 200% and in both themes: the window, the pills, the rows and
    the chip are legible, and nothing overlaps at 960 x 540. (The look reuses the parameters
    window's; say what you would change.)

Record the plugins (RP-n), what you hear, the device, driver and buffer size, and anything that

### MT-AUTO-5 · Controls that follow a lane, and taking a lane over (needs a human and ears)

   plugin's own window while it plays and move the same parameter there: the lane lets go

   not you moving the control).
8. **Instrument.** On a MIDI track with a plugin instrument (RP-b), repeat step 7 with one of

9. **Master and groups.** A volume lane on the master: its fader in the mixer follows and takes

10. **Looks.** At 100%, 150% and 200% and in both themes: the amber ring, the A button, the chip

### MT-AUTO-6 · Recording a lane (needs a human, ears and real hardware)

Use a project with a track that makes a steady sound (a loop) with a volume lane of a few
points (A, then click), and one plugin **effect** whose parameter you can hear (RP-a, anonymous).
Unfold the track's header so its volume slider shows.

1. **The mode.** Click the lane chip: the menu ends with "While the song plays" and Read, Touch,
   Latch and Write. Choose Touch: the chip reads "Volume · Touch" with a red dot, and the lane is
   tinted red. Choose Read: it goes back. The same from **Ctrl+K** ("Automation mode: Touch",
   Latch, Write, Read) on the selected tracks. Close and reopen the project: every track is in

2. **Touch.** Set Touch, press Space, and drag the volume slider up and down, then let go. While
   you hold it the lane does not fight you and the chip has a red outline; when you let go the
   lane takes over again within about a tenth of a second, with no click (listen for one). Read the
   lane: points where you moved, and a short slope back to where the lane was. **Ctrl+Z** once
   undoes the whole pass.
3. **Touch, by wheel and by keys.** While playing, roll the wheel over the slider one notch: a
   short stretch is written and the lane returns. Move it with the arrow keys the same.
4. **Latch.** Set Latch, press Space, nudge the slider once and let go: the level stays where you

5. **Write.** Set Write and press Space with the slider at some level: the lane is overwritten with
   it from where playing started, moved or not (the sound may jump at the start: it is the control's

5b. **Write leaves other lanes alone.** Give the track a second lane (pan) with some points, show
   the volume lane, set Write and play: the pan lane is not changed. Switch the chip to pan and
   Write again: now pan is overwritten and volume is not.
6. **Loop.** Turn the loop on over a few bars, set Latch or Write and play for three passes: each
   pass is its own undo step and the write carries on across the wrap with no gap.
6b. **A first touch with no lane.** On a track whose volume has no lane yet, show the volume lane
   (A), set Touch, play, drag the slider and let go: the fader goes back to where it was before you
   touched it (within a tenth of a second), not left where you let go. The new lane is flat at
   that level with your move in it. Do the same with Latch: it holds your last level.
7. **Not while stopped.** With Touch set, stopped, drag the slider: nothing is written (the lane is
   taken over, as in Read, amber).
8. **A plugin's window.** Choose the effect's parameter for a lane ("Parameters of…" in the chip's
   menu), set Touch, play, open the plugin's own window and drag that parameter: the lane is
   written from the moment you grab it to the moment you let go, and returns. Try a plugin that
   starts a gesture and sends no value until you move, and one that sends values with no gesture.
9. **A hardware or key move.** If a controller is mapped in a later slice, skip. Otherwise move a
   send knob and a pan slider the same way: they write their own lanes.
10. **Accuracy.** Play a click track at 120 BPM at 64 and 128 frames. In Touch, drag the slider
    quickly and compare where the first and last written points sit against where you moved
    (zoom into the lane). Say how many milliseconds the error looks like. (The test device's
    playhead is within 12 ms of straight; the real one should be tighter.)
11. **Looks.** At 100%, 150% and 200%, in both themes: the dot, the chip, the red tint and the
    menu are legible and nothing overlaps at 960 x 540. Is it clear which lanes are armed?

Record the plugins (RP-n), the device, driver and buffer size, and anything that differs from the

### MT-AUTO-7 · MIDI learn (needs a human and a MIDI controller)

Slice A5 of M7 (ADR-0088). **Status: passed (owner, 2026-10-20; reported as working well, no step detail), with a virtual MIDI cable and an on-screen MIDI keyboard sending controller messages, not a hardware controller.** A virtual port is enough for steps 1 to 9, 12 and 14. Not checked with real hardware: the recording delay of step 10, and steps 11 and 13 (a mapped controller kept from a MIDI track; two knobs at once).

Start with `cargo run --release -p lumen-app` (rebuild the app; no host rebuild is needed). Use any controller with knobs or faders that send controller messages (not relative encoders).

1. **Switch the controller on.** Open Audio and MIDI settings, MIDI tab, and switch your controller on for tracks (or remote).
2. **Learn a fader.** Add an audio track. Right-click its volume slider in the header: the menu says "Volume" and offers Automate, Learn a controller…, Controller mappings…. Choose Learn. The slider gets a blue ring and a note says "Move a controller to map … Esc to stop". Move a knob on the controller: the note goes, the slider stays where it was.
3. **It moves the control.** Move the same knob: the slider and the mixer's fader follow it over the whole range, 0 at the bottom and full at +6 dB. Check the controller's other knobs do nothing.
4. **One undo step.** Sweep the knob up and down a few times, then wait a moment. Ctrl+Z: the fader goes back to where it was before the sweep (one step), Ctrl+Z again: the mapping is gone.
5. **Esc.** Choose Learn again, press Esc: the ring and note go and no mapping is made.
6. **Other controls.** Map a pan slider, a send knob (give the track a send to a return first), the mixer strip's trim, the M button (it mutes from the half of the knob up), and a plugin knob on a device card and a row of All parameters. Each learns from its own right-click menu and then follows its knob.
7. **The window.** Open Controller mappings… from any menu, or the palette: every mapping is listed with its control, controller and channel, and a Clear button. Clear one: that knob stops. The last message heard shows as you move knobs. Clear all.
8. **Keyboard only.** Select a track, press A, run "Learn a controller for the shown lane's control" from the palette (Ctrl+K), move a knob: it is mapped. Run "Clear the controller mapping of the shown lane's control": it is cleared.
9. **A lane taken over.** Give the volume a lane (A, click points) and play: the fader follows the lane. Move the mapped knob: the lane is taken over (amber), the knob's value is heard, and Re-enable gives the lane back.
10. **Touch.** Set the track's mode to Touch (the lane chip's menu), play, and sweep the knob: the lane is written while you move it and goes back to the lane about a third of a second after you stop. Do the same in Latch and Write. Say how close the written points sit to where you moved the knob (zoom into the lane); recording uses the frame that applies the move, so a delay of a frame is expected.
11. **A controller that a MIDI track uses.** Map a controller number that your MIDI track's instrument also responds to (the modulation wheel, controller 1): once mapped it should move only the control, not the instrument.
12. **Save and reopen.** Save, close, reopen: the mappings are there (in the window) and the knobs move their controls without learning again. Open an older project: no mappings, nothing else changed.
13. **Many messages.** Move two knobs at once, fast, for ten seconds: nothing stutters. The window shows no lost messages (or says how many were lost).
14. **Looks.** At 100%, 150% and 200%, in both themes: the menu rows, the ring, the note and the window are legible, and nothing overlaps at 960 x 540.
15. **Record a take and a sweep together.** Arm a MIDI track with an instrument, learn a knob on one of its controls (its volume, or a parameter of its plugin) and leave the track in Read. Press Record (F9): a note says automation is on for the track (Latch) and its chip shows the mode. Play notes and move the knob. Stop: the take is a clip with your notes, the volume lane (made by the take, and shown) holds the sweep, the track is back in Read, and Ctrl+Z steps back through the pieces (the take and the lane are separate steps, a known limit). Repeat with the track set to Touch first: it stays Touch.

Record the controller, the device, driver and buffer size, and anything that differs from the above.


### MT-AUTO-8 · Tempo and meter lanes (needs a human and ears)

Slice A6 of M7 (ADR-0089). **Status: passed (owner, 2026-10-22; reported as passed with the rest of M7, no step detail).**
