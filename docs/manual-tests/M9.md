# Audio input, recording, export: manual tests

## MT-REC-1 — Audio input and monitoring (R1)

**State:** Not run yet. Branch: `m9-s1-audio-input`. Needs the Focusrite
interface, once through ASIO and once through WASAPI. Recording itself is R2:
this test checks that input arrives, is routed, metered and heard.

From the repository root:

```powershell
cargo build --release -p lumen-plugin-host
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

Use a scratch project with two audio tracks ("Vox" and "Gtr") and plug a
signal (a microphone or an instrument) into input 1 of the interface.

**ASIO**

1. Open the audio settings and choose the Focusrite's ASIO driver. The Input
   row offers All inputs (chosen), each pair by its friendly name, and None.
   The Latency section shows Input, Output and Round trip; note the figures.
2. Arm Vox and unfold its header (four steps tall since R1b). The In chip sits
   on the third row under pan and names the first free input ("In 1" for a mono
   input). Undo once: the arm and the input go back together.
3. Play into input 1. Vox's meter and the chip's small level bar move; you hear
   the input (Auto monitoring: heard while armed and stopped). Press play: the
   input is still heard while armed and the clips on the track play too.
4. Click the chip: a menu lists the pairs and the mono inputs by name, and None.
   Choose "1+2": the chip says so and the signal comes in stereo. Undo returns
   to the previous input.
5. Arm Gtr: it takes the next free input, not input 1.
6. Overload input 1 for a moment. The chip's clip light stays on after the
   signal is gone; click it to clear it (Alt-click clears every input's light).
7. Unarm Vox: it falls silent. Set its monitor mode to In (the mixer strip,
   or "Next monitor mode" in Ctrl+K): it is heard unarmed, and a Monitor badge
   shows on its top row. Set Off: armed, it is not heard, but its input meter
   still moves.
8. Insert an effect on Vox (any EQ, or a plugin) and speak: the monitored
   signal goes through the effect. Move a control while playing: no click.
   (This is the ADR-0069 trial: if you hear dropouts at 64 or 128 samples
   through a plugin, note the buffer size and the plugin.)
9. Open the input-less state: choose None for the device's inputs in the audio
   settings. The chips say "(not on this device)" for routed tracks; choosing
   All inputs brings them back with no further step.
10. Use Ctrl+K: "Next track input" and "Next monitor mode" with Vox selected
    step through the inputs and Auto, In, Off.

**WASAPI**

11. Choose WASAPI shared and the Focusrite. The Input row lists the inputs of
    the interface's capture endpoints (all of them, in the order of their
    names). Repeat steps 2 to 7.
12. Listen to a held tone for two minutes while monitoring: no click, no change
    in pitch. (A drift correction slips a frame about every few seconds at most;
    the crossfade should not be heard.) Note the Input latency in the audio
    settings.
13. With the interface's driver in exclusive mode (WASAPI exclusive), repeat
    steps 2 and 3. Input is still captured in shared mode beside it.
14. Pull the USB cable while monitoring: the audio panel reports the lost
    device as before, and nothing crashes. Plug it back and use the recovery
    button.

**Report back:** the Input, Output and Round trip figures for ASIO and WASAPI,
the buffer sizes tried, and anything heard that should not be (a click, a
dropout, a pitch change).

## MT-REC-4 — Track headers (R1b)

**State:** Not run yet. Branch: `m9-s1b-track-headers` (ADR-0101, the owner's
B·1). No audio hardware needed: this is a check by eye. The screenshots in
`track_headers` (shots test) show the same scenes.

From the repository root:

```powershell
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

Use a scratch project with three audio tracks, a MIDI track and a group (Ctrl+G
on two of them), and three return tracks.

1. **Folded headers.** Each row shows the type icon, the name, and M, S and the
   arm dot as pieces of one sunk well at the right (a return's well has M and S
   only, and lines up at the right with the others). Nothing touches: the icon,
   the name and the well have even gaps. Under the pointer the icon turns into
   the fold arrow; a group always shows its arrow.
2. **Level strip.** Play: a thin strip beside each colour rail shows the
   track's level, folded or unfolded. Overload a track for a moment: a red mark
   stays at the top of its strip until clicked.
3. **Unfold** an audio track (click its icon, or the arrow under the pointer).
   It opens to 88 pt with three evenly spaced rows: the name with M S ●;
   volume and pan; the send well and the In chip. The name glides up as it
   opens. The left edges of the volume field and the send well line up, and so
   do the right column's pan and In chip.
4. **Send well.** With three returns, the well holds three slots lettered
   A, B, C in their colours. Drag a slot up: it fills from the bottom and a
   flag above it names the return and the level. Double-click: silence.
   Click and type -6, Enter: -6.0 dB. Right-click: pre- or post-fader.
   Hovering a slot lights its return's header.
5. **Twelve returns.** Add returns until there are twelve (L). Every return has
   a slot on the unfolded track's header, in one row inside the left column,
   without letters at the default width; the flag names each. Drag the header
   column wider: the slots grow until their letters show.
6. **In chip.** Choose None, "1+2", then "2" from the chip's menu: the chip does
   not change size or place, "In" stays where it is, and the caret stays at the
   right. With no input it says "None" in grey.
7. **Unfold a return** and the master: a return lists its senders on the third
   row, starting under its volume; the master shows volume and pan.
8. **The A key** (and the track menu's automation entry) shows and hides the
   lane: there is no A button on the header any more.
9. Repeat 1, 3, 5 and 6 in the light theme, and at 150% and 200% UI scale.
   Nothing overlaps or is cut off at the narrowest header (204 pt).
10. Keyboard: Tab reaches the fold button (a focus ring around it), M, S and arm,
    volume, pan, each send slot and the In chip, in that order.

**Report back:** anything crowded, misaligned or hard to read, with the theme
and scale.

## MT-REC-2 — Recording audio (R2)

**State:** Not run yet. Branch: `m9-s2-audio-recording`. Needs the Focusrite
interface (ASIO, then WASAPI). Automated: the end-to-end scenarios in
`tests/e2e/audio_record.rs` (record, loop, punch, save and reopen, kill and
recover) on the fake device with a ramp, so no frame is lost, repeated or moved.
This test is for what only hardware shows: the real latency compensation and a
long take.

From the repository root:

```powershell
cargo build --release -p lumen-plugin-host
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

Use a scratch project with two audio tracks ("Vox" and "Gtr"). Plug a signal
into inputs 1 and 2.

1. Arm Vox and press F9 (Record). A record-red clip with a "REC" tag grows from
   the playhead and its wave moves as you play. Press Space: the clip stays, in
   the track's colour, named "Vox 1". Undo once: the clip goes (the take's file
   stays in `Recordings` beside the autosaves). Redo brings it back.
2. Arm Vox and Gtr (each takes its own input) and record together: each gets a
   clip. Record both with a count-in (the transport's count-in setting): the
   clips begin at the first beat after the count-in.
3. **Latency (the main check).** Play a click or a clap into input 1 while a
   metronome plays through the interface to your headphones. Record, then zoom
   in on the take: the recorded click should sit on the beat within about 1 ms
   (the clip was moved back by the input and output latency). Note the Input,
   Output and Round trip figures, the buffer and the driver. If it is early or
   late, open the audio window and set the **Recording offset** (Latency
   section) by the error in milliseconds (positive moves a take earlier),
   record again, and note the value that is right for this driver.
4. Record with Loop on (a one-bar loop). One long clip results, longer than the
   loop, and it plays on without a gap. Record with punch in and out switched
   on: the clip covers the punched range only.
5. Record for ten minutes. No click or gap on playback; the status bar shows no
   dropouts and the disk notice does not appear.
6. Record with a 64-sample buffer on ASIO, while scrolling and zooming: note any
   dropouts.
7. With Vox recording, try to move or delete one of its clips: the edit is
   refused with a note. Other tracks still edit.
8. Save as a new project: the take is copied into `Samples/Recorded` beside the
   project file; close and reopen it: the clip plays.
9. **Kill and recover.** Record for twenty seconds, then end Lumen from the Task
   Manager. Start Lumen: the recovery banner offers the autosave; choose
   Recover: the clip is back on Vox with a note that Lumen stopped while
   recording. Play it.
10. **Recording over a clip (found by the owner, 2026-10-25).** Record a clip on
    Vox, return to its start and record again on Vox. The old clip is silent
    from where the take begins (you hear only the live input), and when you
    stop it is cut back to where the take ends. Then record with punch in and
    out over the middle of a clip: the clip plays on either side of the punch
    and is silent inside it. Stop with the transport idle and do not touch the
    mouse: the clip appears with its waveform at once (it used to wait for
    the next mouse move or Play). While you record, the old clip is not drawn
    under the growing take (the growing take covers it as far as it has gone; past the take it stays). Compare the
    growing take's wave with the finished clip's: the same shape, the same
    thin line in quiet passages, and for a stereo input the same two halves.
11. Pull the USB cable while recording: Lumen reports the lost device; what was
    written is kept as a clip after the stop.

**Report back:** the figures in step 3 and the offset that was right, the
buffer sizes tried, any dropout or click, and how much the recovered take
lost in step 9.

## MT-REC-3 — Takes and Capture MIDI (R3)

**State:** Not run yet. Branch: `m9-s3-takes-capture`. Needs the Focusrite
interface (for the audio passes) and a MIDI keyboard. Automated: the
end-to-end scenarios in `tests/e2e/audio_record.rs` (loop passes as takes,
swap, undo, save and reopen), `record.rs` (Replace makes takes, Overdub still
layers) and `capture_midi.rs` (stopped, moving, setting off, palette) on the
fake devices. This test is for what only hardware shows: whether the passes
line up by ear, and Capture MIDI from a real keyboard.

From the repository root:

```powershell
cargo build --release -p lumen-plugin-host
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

Use a scratch project with an audio track "Vox" (input 1) and a MIDI track
with an instrument.

1. **Audio passes.** Set a two-bar loop and switch Loop on. Arm Vox, press F9
   and play or sing something different on each of four passes, then press
   Space. While you record, the red clip is the length of the loop. At each wrap
   the new pass is drawn from the loop's start over the pass before it, which
   stays dimmed behind it until the new one has replaced it; nothing runs on
   past the loop and nothing disappears. After Stop the clip stays on screen
   until the placed clip takes over (no empty moment). Stop
   a good way into the fifth pass: one clip covers the loop (as long as the
   loop, not as long as the last, partial pass) and shows **5/5** in its
   label. It plays the last pass that lasted the whole loop (Take 4); Take 5 is
   the partial one and is silent after where you stopped. Stop again within a
   quarter of a second of a wrap: there is no partial take, the chip counts
   whole passes only.
2. Right-click the clip: **Takes (4)** lists "Take 1 of 4" to "Take 4 of 4" with
   "(playing)" on the current one, then Play next take, Play previous take, an
   Extract to new track submenu and Keep only this take. Choose Take 2: the clip plays that pass
   and the chip reads 2/4. Undo once: Take 4 plays again.
3. **Alignment (the main check).** With a click or a held note on the beat in
   each pass, listen to Take 1 and Take 3 against the metronome: each should
   sit on the beat as the single take of MT-REC-2 did (note the offset you set
   there). Note any pass that is early or late, and by how much.
4. Use the keyboard only: select the clip and use the command palette
   (Ctrl+K) for "Play next take" and "Play previous take". Their keycaps in the
   Takes menu come from the keymap.
5. Choose Extract to new track, then Take 1: a copy of the clip appears on a new track
   after Vox, playing the first pass, and Vox is unchanged. One undo step.
6. Keep only this take: the chip goes and the clip is a plain clip. Undo brings
   the takes back. Split a clip with takes (Ctrl+E): a notice says which take
   was kept and that the others were removed; Undo brings them back.
7. **Recording over a clip that reaches outside the loop.** Put a clip on Vox that
   starts before the loop and runs past its end (or move the clip so it is
   partly outside the loop), set the loop over part of it, and loop-record on
   Vox, starting from the start of the song and again from inside the loop. While
   you record, the old clip is silent inside the loop. After Stop the new clip is
   exactly the loop long and sits on the loop; the part of the old clip before
   and after the loop is still there and plays; nothing of the old clip plays
   under the new one.
   Then switch **Punch in at the loop start** off (the loop stays on), put a clip
   across the loop's start and record from before it: the old clip is silent from
   the moment you press record (no pop at the start), not only from the loop;
   after Stop what you played before the loop is a clip called "... lead-in"
   where you played it, and the old clip is gone under it.
8. Save, close and reopen the project: the chip and every take are still there.
9. **MIDI passes.** On the MIDI track, with Loop on and the record mode on
   Replace, play a different phrase on each of three passes. One clip with
   **3/3** results and the Takes menu works as for audio (except Extract, which
   is not listed). Switch to Overdub and record again: the passes layer into
   one clip with no takes, as before.
10. **Capture MIDI, stopped.** Arm the MIDI track, make sure the transport is
   stopped and play a phrase on the keyboard (no recording). Press Shift+F9: a
   clip appears at the playhead, the first note on the playhead, with a toast
   "Captured n notes on <track> at bar b. Undo takes it away." Undo removes it.
   Pressing Shift+F9 again captures nothing.
11. **Capture MIDI, moving.** Press Space and play along to a loop of other
    tracks without recording. Stop and press Shift+F9: the notes land where you
    played them against the song, not at the playhead.
12. Switch **Keep played notes for Capture MIDI** off (the record menu on the transport bar, or the
    palette). Play a phrase: Shift+F9 says why it cannot run. Switch it on, play,
    and it works.
13. Record a take normally, then press Shift+F9 straight away: nothing new is
    captured (what was recorded is not offered again).

**Report back:** any pass that was out of time and by how much (step 3), and
anything in steps 10 to 13 that put a note in the wrong place.
