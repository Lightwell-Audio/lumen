# Audio input, recording, export, preferences: manual tests

## MT-AUDIO-7 — Switching driver model without freezing (fix before R7b)

**State:** Not run. Branch: `fix/async-device-change`, ADR-0113. Needs the
Focusrite. Lumen keeps your own audio settings: note the driver and device
you start on and put them back at the end.

```powershell
cargo build --release -p lumen-plugin-host
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

1. Open **Preferences…** on the Audio page. Start on Windows audio
   (shared) with the Focusrite, or choose it.
2. Choose **ASIO**, then **Focusrite USB ASIO**, and at once drag the
   Preferences window around by its title row for a few seconds. The window
   keeps moving smoothly and the song's playhead keeps drawing. The row
   under the device says what is happening ("Closing Windows audio
   (shared)…", then "Starting Focusrite USB ASIO…"), and the driver, device,
   format and channel controls are greyed until the device runs; hovering
   one says why.
3. While they are greyed, try to choose another device: nothing happens.
4. Once it runs, choose **Windows audio (shared)** and the Focusrite again,
   dragging the window the same way. Then switch to ASIO and back once more
   while a song plays.
5. With the keyboard only: Tab to the Buffer size or Device menu, change it
   with Enter, an arrow and Enter. When the device has answered, the menu
   has the keyboard again (its focus ring shows).

**Report back:** any moment the window stopped moving or the playhead
froze, how long each switch took, and whether any message ended in "…" or
ran out of its row.

## MT-PREF-1 — Preferences, tap tempo and the new commands (R7)

**State:** Not run. Branch: `m9-s7-preferences`, ADR-0112. Needs the
Focusrite for step 3. Lumen keeps your own settings: note any you change and
put them back at the end.

```powershell
cargo build --release -p lumen-plugin-host
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

1. Open **Preferences…** from the app menu (click **Lumen** at the left of
   the bar), then close it and open it again with Ctrl+K, "Preferences".
   Look at every page in both themes at 100 and 200% (Appearance page).
   Make the window 960 × 540 at 200%: the page list folds into a Page menu
   and nothing runs out of the window.
2. Type **latency**, **dark** and **folder** in the search. Each lists its
   settings with their pages; Enter or a click opens the page with the row
   outlined.
3. On the Audio page, change the buffer, then the device, and back. On
   Appearance, change the theme. Each applies at once. Close and restart
   Lumen: they are kept.
4. Plugins page: **Add** a folder, **Remove** it, and Rescan. If a plugin is
   blocked, it is listed with **Retry**.
5. Editing page: set **Autosave every 30 seconds**. Make an edit, wait
   40 s, then end Lumen from Task Manager. Start it again: the recovery
   offers work no more than 30 s old.
6. Editing page: set **Undo steps** to 1,000. The row says it is used from
   the next project; File ▸ New, and it is. Set new audio tracks to monitor
   **In** and new MIDI tracks to **None**; add one of each: the audio track's
   monitor is In and the MIDI track has no instrument. Put both back.
7. Files page: **Choose…** a projects folder. Save as and Open start there.
   Press **Standard** to go back.
8. Files page: the cache shows how much it holds; **Clear cache** empties it
   (a playing song keeps playing). Choose another cache folder: the row says
   it is used from the next start. Restart: Files offers the old folder with
   **Clear old cache** and **Keep it**. Choose **Standard** again and restart.
9. Tap tempo: press Play, then click **Tap** after the tempo in time with
   the song, about eight times. The piece counts the taps; from the third the
   tempo follows and is heard at once. Wait three seconds: the piece says Tap
   again. One Ctrl+Z restores the old tempo. Bind Tap tempo to a key in
   Keyboard shortcuts and tap with it; holding the key down taps once.
10. Ctrl+K: run Loop on or off, Metronome on or off, Go to end (and End),
    Audio on or off, Next time format, Show or hide the browser, Solo or Cue,
    Add return track and Add group track.

**Report back:** anything that did not apply at once or was not kept after a
restart, whether tapping felt steady (the tempo a whole BPM, no jumps), and
any page or row that looked crowded or unclear in either theme.

## MT-FIRSTRUN-1 — The first-run window (R7b)

**State:** Not run. Branch: `m9-s7b-first-run`, ADR-0115. Needs the
Focusrite. This test uses the real audio device on purpose. Every per-user
file goes to a new scratch folder, and `%APPDATA%\Lumen` is never touched.
First, once:

```powershell
cargo build --release -p lumen-plugin-host
$dir = "$env:TEMP\lumen-first-run-$(Get-Date -Format yyyyMMdd-HHmmss)"
New-Item -ItemType Directory -Force $dir | Out-Null
```

Then start Lumen, now and again in step 5, with this line only:

```powershell
cargo run --release -p lumen-app -- --settings "$dir\settings.json" --autosave-dir "$dir\autosave" --crash-dir "$dir\crash" --user-dir "$dir\user"
```

1. The window opens on **Sound**, with Lumi waving, and offers the
   Focusrite's ASIO driver ("… is installed. It records with less delay").
2. **Play test sound** on Windows audio: a soft chime on the left, then the
   right, then both, in the right speakers, while the L and R lights follow
   it. Say whether it is too loud or too quiet.
3. **Use ASIO.** While it switches, the window still answers (drag it, or
   open a menu behind it). Then the round trip shows under the sound check,
   and the test sound plays through the interface.
4. Go to **Plugins** and add a folder while the first scan is still
   running: "Waiting to check …" shows, and the folder is checked after.
   Go to **Ready**, pick **Light** and **150%**, and press **Start making
   music**.
5. Close Lumen and start it again with the launch line. The window does not
   open, and the ASIO device, the folder, the theme and the size are kept.
6. **Set up Lumen…**, from the app menu (under Preferences…) and from the
   palette (Ctrl+K, "set up"), opens the window again on Sound.
7. With the keyboard only: Tab walks the step's controls, Enter on Next
   moves on, Esc with a menu open closes the menu, and Esc again closes
   the window.

**Report back:** how the chime sounded, any moment the window stopped
answering, and any text that ran out of its row or ended in "…".

## MT-REC-5 — The loopback helper (R2b)

**State:** Not run. Branch: `m9-s2b-loopback-helper`, ADR-0110. Needs the
Focusrite and one short instrument cable (jack to jack, or line out to a
line or instrument input). Turn the monitors and headphones **down** first:
the measurement plays five loud ticks on the output it uses.

```powershell
cargo build --release -p lumen-plugin-host
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

1. Run a cable from **output 1** (or a line output of pair 1/2) to
   **input 2**. Set input 2's gain to about a quarter on the interface.
2. Open the audio settings: the Focusrite's ASIO driver, 48 kHz, 128
   samples. In **Latency**, press **Measure…** beside Recording offset.
   A card unfolds under it. Choose **Cable from** 1/2 and **Cable to** 2,
   then press **Measure**. You hear five ticks over about 1.5 s, and nothing
   else Lumen plays while they sound. The strip shows the returned click
   as one sharp line with "5 of 5 agree".
3. Read **Measured round trip** and "driver says". Press **Use +x ms**. The
   Recording offset field shows the value, and the button becomes "The
   recording offset is +x ms". Press **Measure again** twice: the number
   stays within a sample or two.
4. Unplug the cable and press **Measure again**. The card says nothing came
   back on input 2 and what to check. Plug it back in.
5. Turn input 2's gain to full and measure. If the input clips, the card
   says so and still gives the same number.
6. Set the buffer to **256 samples** in the ASIO control panel. Under the
   offset, a hint now says "Measured with ASIO · … at 48.0 kHz, 128
   samples. Measure again." Measure and use the result: the hint goes.
7. Switch the driver to **Windows audio (shared)**. Choose an output pair
   and the cable's input in the card, and measure. Note the measured and
   reported figures: WASAPI's input figure is an estimate (KNOWN_ISSUES).
8. Back on ASIO with the measured offset in use: arm an audio track on
   input 2 (cable still in), put a short clip with a sharp attack (a
   clap or a click) on another track routed to output 1/2, and record over
   it. Zoom in on the take: its attack lines up with the clip's to within a
   few samples.
9. Ctrl+K, type "Measure recording latency": the command opens the settings
   at the card. Press Play: Measure is held with "Stop playback to measure".
10. Look at the card in both themes at 100, 150 and 200%: nothing overlaps
    or runs out of the window, and every message is read in full.

**Report back:** the measured and reported round trips on ASIO (128 and
256) and WASAPI, the spread over three runs, whether the take in step 8
lined up (by how many samples if not), and anything the card said that was
unclear.

## MT-STREAM-1 — Playing audio from disk (R6)

**State:** Passed (owner, 2026-10-28). Branch: `m9-s6-disk-streaming`, ADR-0109.
Needs the Focusrite on ASIO, and about 2.5 GB free on D:. The test project
is 64 tracks of three-minute files, each a quiet two-tone chord of its own,
so a gap in any track is heard as a change in the sound.

From the repository root, write the project once (about half a minute):

```powershell
$env:LUMEN_STREAM_TEST_DIR = "D:\LumenStreamTest"
cargo test --release -p lumen-app --test e2e make_the_streaming_test_project -- --ignored
Remove-Item Env:LUMEN_STREAM_TEST_DIR
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

1. Open the audio settings: the Focusrite's ASIO driver, 48 kHz,
   **128 samples** (Buffer size). Turn on **Play audio from disk** (below Test tone). Open
   `D:\LumenStreamTest\Streaming test.lumen`. The 64 tracks load in a few
   seconds and show their waveforms. The audio panel (click the status
   chip) shows **Clip audio in memory** in the low hundreds of MB at most,
   and a **Disk** row ("0% · 0 late").
2. Play from the start for two minutes. The sound is steady; the Disk row
   stays at **0 late** and the status shows no chip. Note the CPU figure.
3. While playing: set a loop of two bars and let it go round for a minute;
   it wraps without a gap. Drag the loop elsewhere while it plays. Click the
   ruler to jump ten times, to places near and far; each jump starts at
   once, or after a pause too short to notice. Add three markers and jump
   between them with the marker keys.
4. Still playing, edit for five minutes: move clips across tracks, split
   (Ctrl+E), duplicate (Ctrl+D), mute and solo tracks, change the tempo,
   undo and redo. The sound follows each edit and never stops.
5. Leave it playing, looping or not, for **30 minutes in all**, then read the
   Disk row and the Dropouts row. Press **Reset** in the panel.
6. Set **64 samples** and repeat steps 2 to 5 (a shorter run is fine if 128
   was clean: 10 minutes).
7. Turn **Play audio from disk** off. The files load again into memory
   (Clip audio in memory rises to about 4 GB); the Disk row goes. Play: the
   project sounds the same as from disk. Turn it on again.
8. Close Lumen and start it again with the project: the waveforms appear at
   once (their peaks are in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Lumen\Cache`). Reverse a clip
   (its reversed copy is made in the cache) and play it.
9. If "Disk late" ever shows: hover it and click it. The tooltip and the
   panel say what happened and what helps, in full, in both themes at 100,
   150 and 200%.

**Report back:** the buffer sizes run, how long each ran, the Disk row's
late count and the Dropouts count at the end of each, the CPU figure, any
gap or click heard (when, and what was happening), how long the project
took to open the first and the second time, and the size of the Cache
folder. Afterwards `D:\LumenStreamTest` can be deleted, and the Cache
folder too (it is made again as needed).

**Run 1 (owner, 2026-10-28, the Focusrite on ASIO):** passed. No issues at
64 or at 128 samples. The figures asked for above (run lengths, late and
dropout counts, CPU, open times, Cache size) were not reported.

**Automated runs:** the soak (`disk/soak.rs`: 64 streamed tracks in real
time with uneven reads, loops and locates; 0 late in 16 s and 120 s) and
the E2E scenarios in `crates/lumen-app/tests/e2e/streaming.rs`; the
measurements are in ADR-0109.

## MT-EXPORT-1 — Export master, stems and device-driven audio (R5)

**State:** Pending owner run. Branch: `m9-s5-audio-export`, ADR-0107.
Use a scratch song with recorded audio, a MIDI instrument, a group, a return
with an audible send, automation and an effect that rings after the song.
Keep the export folder separate from the project's media.

From the repository root:

```powershell
cargo build --release -p lumen-plugin-host
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

1. Open **Export audio…** from the app menu, the command palette, then
   **Ctrl+Shift+E** (Cmd on macOS). Esc closes it. The previous editor-pop-out
   shortcut now adds Alt/Option. Check keyboard focus, dropdown arrow keys,
   Tab/Shift+Tab and Enter on the Export button. Choices survive reopening
   the window during this session.
2. Export **Whole project**, **Master mix**, **WAV · 32-bit float**, at the
   project's rate, **Offline**, with tails on. The song, its automation,
   group, return and master processing are heard; the tail follows the end.
   The project, unsaved mark and undo history stay as they were. The report
   names the file, duration, integrated LUFS and true peak. Silence and files
   shorter than 400 ms say why no integrated reading is available.
3. Set a loop shorter than the song; choose **Loop region**, tails off, and
   export. The result starts at the loop's left edge and ends at its right,
   without repeated loops. Draw a time selection inside clips, choose
   **Time selection**, and export: only that span is in the file. Clearing
   the time selection disables export for that range with a message.
4. Export **WAV · 16-bit**, **WAV · 24-bit**, **FLAC · 16-bit** and
   **FLAC · 24-bit**. Open each file elsewhere and inspect its rate and bit
   depth. Try 44.1 and 96 kHz from a 48 kHz project; duration stays the same.
   Enable **TPDF dither** on an integer file and **Normalize peak to −1 dBFS**.
   The sample peak is −1 dBFS; the reported true peak may be higher.
5. Choose **Track stems**, then **Group stems**, then **Return stems**, each
   into a fresh folder. Files share their starting sample and range. Track
   stems include their own devices/fader/pan, group stems include children,
   and return stems contain incoming sends. Sends leaving that bus and its
   downstream group/master processing are outside that stem. Mute and solo
   are respected. Turn normalize off before summing stems for comparison.
6. Choose a folder already holding these stems: the task explains the
   collision and preserves the files. Try the path of imported audio as a
   master destination: it is refused. Export a master over an earlier export:
   successful completion replaces it. Cancel a long export and confirm that
   the earlier file remains and no new partial export is published.
7. Choose **Real-time**, at matching project/device rates, with the Focusrite
   ASIO device running at 128 frames, then 64. Playback stops and the snapshot
   is heard through the main outputs while it exports. Monitoring, live MIDI
   and metronome are excluded. Recording is unavailable during this task.
   Compare with the offline export and record whether an external plugin
   behaves differently. Repeat with tails off and on. Cancel once by the
   button, once by Esc. A stopped or unavailable device should report a
   failure rather than publish missing audio.
8. While an offline export runs, move or mute a clip: its file continues from
   the starting snapshot; the edit stays in the open project. Try export
   while an external MIDI instrument's track has notes in the range: the
   message asks for its sound to be recorded to audio first. A missing plugin
   or missing media has an actionable failure, rather than a replacement
   sound presented as successful export.
9. Check the window and the report in both themes at **100%, 150%, 200%**,
   including a 960 × 540 point window. Scroll down to remaining controls
   and the loudness report. No label or button should be cut off or overlap.

**Report back:** interface/rate/buffer, formats opened, files and durations,
whether tails and stems sounded right, offline/real-time differences, and any
cut-off text or keyboard-focus problem. External plugin records use consistent
anonymous IDs with version and settings. Do not infer broad compatibility from
one passing plugin.

**Automated runs:** documented in `../milestones/M9.md` (§4, R5); these use isolated
fake devices, temporary settings/projects and the synthetic test plugin.

## MT-REC-1 — Audio input and monitoring (R1)

**State:** Passed (owner, 2026-10-26; reported as "looks good", no step detail and no latency figures). Branch: `m9-s1-audio-input`. Needs the Focusrite
interface, once through ASIO and once through WASAPI. Recording itself is R2:
this test checks that input arrives, is routed, metered and heard.

From the repository root:

```powershell
cargo build --release -p lumen-plugin-host
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

Use a scratch project with two audio tracks ("Vox" and "Gtr") and plug a
signal (a microphone or an instrument) into input 1 of the interface.

**ASIO**

1. Open the audio settings and choose the Focusrite's ASIO driver. The Input
   row offers All inputs (chosen), each pair by its friendly name, and None.
   The Latency section shows Input, Output and Round trip; note the figures.
2. Arm Vox and unfold its header (four steps tall since R1b). The In chip sits
   on the third row under pan and names the first free input ("In 1" for a mono
   input). Undo once: the arm and the input go back together.
3. Play into input 1. Vox's meter and the chip's small level bar move; you hear
   the input (Auto monitoring: heard while armed and stopped). Press play: the
   input is still heard while armed and the clips on the track play too.
4. Click the chip: a menu lists the pairs and the mono inputs by name, and None.
   Choose "1+2": the chip says so and the signal comes in stereo. Undo returns
   to the previous input.
5. Arm Gtr: it takes the next free input, not input 1.
6. Overload input 1 for a moment. The chip's clip light stays on after the
   signal is gone; click it to clear it (Alt-click clears every input's light).
7. Unarm Vox: it falls silent. Set its monitor mode to In (the mixer strip,
   or "Next monitor mode" in Ctrl+K): it is heard unarmed, and a Monitor badge
   shows on its top row. Set Off: armed, it is not heard, but its input meter
   still moves.
8. Insert an effect on Vox (any EQ, or a plugin) and speak: the monitored
   signal goes through the effect. Move a control while playing: no click.
   (This is the ADR-0069 trial: if you hear dropouts at 64 or 128 samples
   through a plugin, note the buffer size and the plugin.)
9. Open the input-less state: choose None for the device's inputs in the audio
   settings. The chips say "(not on this device)" for routed tracks; choosing
   All inputs brings them back with no further step.
10. Use Ctrl+K: "Next track input" and "Next monitor mode" with Vox selected
    step through the inputs and Auto, In, Off.

**WASAPI**

11. Choose WASAPI shared and the Focusrite. The Input row lists the inputs of
    the interface's capture endpoints (all of them, in the order of their
    names). Repeat steps 2 to 7.
12. Listen to a held tone for two minutes while monitoring: no click, no change
    in pitch. (A drift correction slips a frame about every few seconds at most;
    the crossfade should not be heard.) Note the Input latency in the audio
    settings.
13. With the interface's driver in exclusive mode (WASAPI exclusive), repeat
    steps 2 and 3. Input is still captured in shared mode beside it.
14. Pull the USB cable while monitoring: the audio panel reports the lost
    device as before, and nothing crashes. Plug it back and use the recovery
    button.

**Report back:** the Input, Output and Round trip figures for ASIO and WASAPI,
the buffer sizes tried, and anything heard that should not be (a click, a
dropout, a pitch change).

## MT-REC-4 — Track headers (R1b)

**State:** Passed (owner, 2026-10-26; reported as "looks good", no step detail;
run as the short pass: folded and unfolded headers, send well, twelve returns,
In chip, the A key, light theme at 150% and 200%, Tab order). Branch:
`m9-s1b-track-headers` (ADR-0101, the owner's
B·1). No audio hardware needed: this is a check by eye. The screenshots in
`track_headers` (shots test) show the same scenes.

From the repository root:

```powershell
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

Use a scratch project with three audio tracks, a MIDI track and a group (Ctrl+G
on two of them), and three return tracks.

1. **Folded headers.** Each row shows the type icon, the name, and M, S and the
   arm dot as pieces of one sunk well at the right (a return's well has M and S
   only, and lines up at the right with the others). Nothing touches: the icon,
   the name and the well have even gaps. Under the pointer the icon turns into
   the fold arrow; a group always shows its arrow.
2. **Level strip.** Play: a thin strip beside each colour rail shows the
   track's level, folded or unfolded. Overload a track for a moment: a red mark
   stays at the top of its strip until clicked.
3. **Unfold** an audio track (click its icon, or the arrow under the pointer).
   It opens to 88 pt with three evenly spaced rows: the name with M S ●;
   volume and pan; the send well and the In chip. The name glides up as it
   opens. The left edges of the volume field and the send well line up, and so
   do the right column's pan and In chip.
4. **Send well.** With three returns, the well holds three slots lettered
   A, B, C in their colours. Drag a slot up: it fills from the bottom and a
   flag above it names the return and the level. Double-click: silence.
   Click and type -6, Enter: -6.0 dB. Right-click: pre- or post-fader.
   Hovering a slot lights its return's header.
5. **Twelve returns.** Add returns until there are twelve (L). Every return has
   a slot on the unfolded track's header, in one row inside the left column,
   without letters at the default width; the flag names each. Drag the header
   column wider: the slots grow until their letters show.
6. **In chip.** Choose None, "1+2", then "2" from the chip's menu: the chip does
   not change size or place, "In" stays where it is, and the caret stays at the
   right. With no input it says "None" in grey.
7. **Unfold a return** and the master: a return lists its senders on the third
   row, starting under its volume; the master shows volume and pan.
8. **The A key** (and the track menu's automation entry) shows and hides the
   lane: there is no A button on the header any more.
9. Repeat 1, 3, 5 and 6 in the light theme, and at 150% and 200% UI scale.
   Nothing overlaps or is cut off at the narrowest header (204 pt).
10. Keyboard: Tab reaches the fold button (a focus ring around it), M, S and arm,
    volume, pan, each send slot and the In chip, in that order.

**Report back:** anything crowded, misaligned or hard to read, with the theme
and scale.

## MT-REC-2 — Recording audio (R2)

**State:** Passed (owner, 2026-10-26; reported as "looks good", no step detail, no latency figures and no recording offset reported). Branch: `m9-s2-audio-recording`. Needs the Focusrite
interface (ASIO, then WASAPI). Automated: the end-to-end scenarios in
`tests/e2e/audio_record.rs` (record, loop, punch, save and reopen, kill and
recover) on the fake device with a ramp, so no frame is lost, repeated or moved.
This test is for what only hardware shows: the real latency compensation and a
long take.

From the repository root:

```powershell
cargo build --release -p lumen-plugin-host
cargo run --release -p lumen-app
```

Use a scratch project with two audio tracks ("Vox" and "Gtr"). Plug a signal
into inputs 1 and 2.

1. Arm Vox and press F9 (Record). A record-red clip with a "REC" tag grows from
   the playhead and its wave moves as you play. Press Space: the clip stays, in
   the track's colour, named "Vox 1". Undo once: the clip goes (the take's file
   stays in `Recordings` beside the autosaves). Redo brings it back.
2. Arm Vox and Gtr (each takes its own input) and record together: each gets a
   clip. Record both with a count-in (the transport's count-in setting): the
   clips begin at the first beat after the count-in.
3. **Latency (the main check).** Play a click or a clap into input 1 while a
   metronome plays through the interface to your headphones. Record, then zoom
   in on the take: the recorded click should sit on the beat within about 1 ms
   (the clip was moved back by the input and output latency). Note the Input,
   Output and Round trip figures, the buffer and the driver. If it is early or
   late, open the audio window and set the **Recording offset** (Latency
   section) by the error in milliseconds (positive moves a take earlier),
   record again, and note the value that is right for this driver.
4. Record with Loop on (a one-bar loop). One long clip results, longer than the
   loop, and it plays on without a gap. Record with punch in and out switched
   on: the clip covers the punched range only.
5. Record for ten minutes. No click or gap on playback; the status bar shows no
   dropouts and the disk notice does not appear.
6. Record with a 64-sample buffer on ASIO, while scrolling and zooming: note any
   dropouts.
7. With Vox recording, try to move or delete one of its clips: the edit is
   refused with a note. Other tracks still edit.
8. Save as a new project: the take is copied into `Samples/Recorded` beside the
   project file; close and reopen it: the clip plays.
9. **Kill and recover.** Record for twenty seconds, then end Lumen from the Task
   Manager. Start Lumen: the recovery banner offers the autosave; choose
   Recover: the clip is back on Vox with a note that Lumen stopped while
   recording. Play it.
10. **Recording over a clip (found by the owner, 2026-10-25).** Record a clip on
    Vox, return to its start and record again on Vox. The old clip is silent
    from where the take begins (you hear only the live input), and when you
    stop it is cut back to where the take ends. Then record with punch in and
    out over the middle of a clip: the clip plays on either side of the punch
    and is silent inside it. Stop with the transport idle and do not touch the
    mouse: the clip appears with its waveform at once (it used to wait for
    the next mouse move or Play). While you record, the old clip is not drawn
    under the growing take (the growing take covers it as far as it has gone; past the take it stays). Compare the
    growing take's wave with the finished clip's: the same shape, the same
    thin line in quiet passages, and for a stereo input the same two halves.
11. Pull the USB cable while recording: Lumen reports the lost device; what was
    written is kept as a clip after the stop.

**Report back:** the figures in step 3 and the offset that was right, the
buffer sizes tried, any dropout or click, and how much the recovered take
lost in step 9.
